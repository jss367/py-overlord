Free-gain rules and policy evaluation · October 2026

Ironworks and Engineer: Contextual Gains

Both cards now separate free gains from purchases while inheriting existing purchase priorities by default. The measured alternatives did not justify replacing those defaults. Engineer continues to keep itself unless a strategy overrides its self-trash decision.

The study contains 720 screen games, 2,400 games on fresh validation seeds, and 2,000 registered-strategy compatibility games. Card-rule changes have separate physical regression tests.

Choosing free gains

Use free_gain_priority=None to inherit purchases, an explicit priority list to choose different free gains, or [] to use the shared contextual ranking. Existing conditional purchase rules and phase-specific strategies still apply under inheritance. Each card retains its prior mandatory fallback if the inherited selector declines. A custom choose_free_gain(state, player, choices, context) can select any legal offered card. Invalid responses get a legal mandatory fallback; empty menus request no choice.

strategy.gain_priority = [PriorityRule("Silver")]
strategy.free_gain_priority = [
    PriorityRule("Village", PriorityRule.max_in_deck("Village", 2)),
    PriorityRule("Smithy", PriorityRule.max_in_deck("Smithy", 1)),
    PriorityRule("Silver"),
]

Context includes the source, initial destination, current hand, complete owned-card counts, game stage and sacrifice. New optional fields identify the physical source_card, gain_number, previous_gain and whether the source can still be trashed. Stored and exiled cards count as owned.

Engineer declares the optional self-trash opportunity with its first gain. Its existing should_trash_engineer_for_extra_gains override runs after that gain and its reactions. After a successful self-trash, the second gain gets a rebuilt legal menu, the actual first gained card after replacement, and Engineer as the sacrifice. A virtual or already-trashed Engineer still gains once per play but cannot trash itself for another gain.

Rules and physical execution

Both menus use modified coin costs and reject Potion or Debt components, unavailable covered cards and non-Supply piles. The shared executor removes the exposed member of each physical pile exactly once and preserves ordinary gain reactions. Ironworks pays every matching live-type bonus, including Capitalism, Enlightenment and active Inheritance, with combinations covered by tests. Snowy Village suppresses its Action bonus.

Trader replacement determines which card was actually gained. Watchtower movement or trashing leaves Ironworks' matching bonuses intact. A later Changeling exchange is separate from gaining, so Ironworks and committed teacher examples retain the original gain identity. Engineer's second menu reflects depletion and trash reactions. Teacher examples snapshot each final legal choice before that gain; failed choices, replacement by another card and speculative pair projections do not create a matching gain label. No training decision vocabulary was added.

Official rules: Intrigue rules, Ironworks on page 5 describe the matching type bonuses; Empires rules, Engineer on page 9 describe the first gain followed by optional self-trash and a second gain. The audit is scoped to these paths, not every possible Dominion interaction.

Frozen diagnostic policies and protocol

The inherited arm uses fixed purchase priorities and keeps Engineer. The diagnostic Ironworks arm uses independent contextual gain values with live immediate bonuses, stranded Actions, ownership and endgame points. The Engineer diagnostic projects complete gain pairs and considers self-trash at the endgame or with at least three owned Engineers, only when the next legal gain has positive heuristic value. It reassesses the actual extra gain after reactions. Pair projections themselves do not predict reactions, future engine turns or every card's text.

Both arms use identical purchases, hand Action order and current rules. Each card faces Smithy money, a Village/Laboratory engine and a Chapel/Witch attack on a ten-card diagnostic kingdom. The only varied policy families are free-gain selection and Engineer's pair/self-trash decision. This is a fixed-policy comparison with no optimizer genes.

The screen uses 30 independent seeds per row starting at 1,412,000. Before validation, the rule selects the diagnostic only if every opponent's screen change has a positive lower confidence bound; otherwise it retains the inherited arm. Both cards selected inheritance. Those recommendations were locked before 100 fresh seeds per row starting at 1,612,000. Each seed runs both policies and both seats. Rejected alternatives remain in validation so regressions are visible. Rows are offset by 10,000; the seed sets are disjoint.

The paired score-margin normal intervals are recorded with the raw data; they are approximate. Retain inherited priorities and the conservative keep-Engineer default. These results do not prove that every contextual policy is weak, that inherited policies are optimal, or that any single Engineer timing rule caused the regression.

Win share awards half a win for equal points and equal turns taken. Confidence bounds use the independent seed as one cluster, averaging the two seats; 95% two-sided Hoeffding intervals have half-widths of 49.6 percentage points on screen and 27.2 on validation. They remain nonzero at zero observed variance. Bounds are per comparison, without correction for multiple comparisons. Equal initial seeds can diverge into different later draws after different decisions. No games were capped in these panels. Registered strategies on their boards

Five registered policies were reevaluated against Smithy money on their own boards, with 100 new seeds, both seats and both selectors: 2,000 games. Both arms retain each policy's existing purchase and self-trash choices and share the corrected rules. This checks selector compatibility; it does not recreate earlier rules defects or measure a policy-strength improvement.

Reproduction and limits

The runner rejects existing output files, stale simulation or runner inputs, and screen/validation seed overlap. It records the summary helper's hash and verifies that inputs and selection evidence remain unchanged during evaluation. Choose fresh output paths when reproducing. Earlier strategy studies and tournament standings are preserved; this study does not replace a global tournament.
