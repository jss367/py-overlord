Remaining Card Decisions: Rules and Tactical Evaluation

Fix the rules and strategy callbacks; retain existing tactical preferences. This study screened fourteen diagnostic alternatives, froze every selection, and validated on fresh seeds. No replacement policy established a consistent improvement across all three opponents.

Scope: 8,400 screening games, 16,800 fresh validation games and 4,080 registered-strategy games across two engine versions: 29,280 games total. There are 112 recorded card-play scenarios covering early building, constrained hands, excessive copies and endgame. This completes the named follow-up panel in #344; it does not audit all 610 registered card names.

Rules and strategy access

  • Courtier: its physical reveal menu now reaches strategy overrides. A nonempty hand must reveal a card; malformed bonus responses receive distinct legal fallbacks. Count current types, including Charlatan, Capitalism, Enlightenment and Inheritance's Action–Command–Victory Estate. Its Action bonus respects Snowy Village. Issue #421; Intrigue rules.
  • Rogue: an eligible card already in the trash requires a gain and suppresses the attack. Otherwise it attacks without gaining newly trashed cards. Both branches exclude Potion/Debt costs. The trash-gain callback now reaches strategies. Trader now exchanges the original gain through the owner-ordered event, returning the physical target to its ordinary, ordered or present non-Supply pile; decline an impossible return. Issue #422.
  • Knights: resolve victims starting to the attacker's left. Finish each victim's discard and source self-trash triggers before the next victim; continue attacking after the source leaves. Exclude Potion/Debt costs. The audit covers the shared attack, not every member's extra ability. Issue #423; Dark Ages rules.
  • Artificer: strategies may choose physical discards independently and then choose an optional gain from a refreshed exact-cost menu. The existing gain-budget hook remains supported, including its junk-first baseline. Discard reactions can change prices or consume a pile; the original gain plan cannot bypass the fresh menu. Zero discards may gain a free card, and discards may gain nothing. Issue #424; Adventures rules.
  • Stables and Spice Merchant: Treasure menus use the shared live-type predicate, and their conditional Action bonuses respect Snowy Village. Issue #425; Hinterlands rules.
  • Counterfeit: validate the physical target against the shared live Treasure menu and resolve both plays through shared Treasure handling. Exclude Duration targets under the 2022 wording and only trash a target still in play; returned Spoils stays returned. Online publisher errata and card image. Issue #426.

Current Inheritance adds Command instructions to Estate; it does not copy the set-aside Action's types or Capitalism qualification. Courtier's count and these three Treasure menus respect that distinction. The wider Inheritance execution and gain-type migration remains open in #427. Existing overlay helpers and gain-type accounting elsewhere are not certified by this audit.

Additional type-classification limit: the shared Capitalism predicate still recognizes immediate printed coin stats only. Conditional/delayed +$ instructions, including Courtier, Minion, Moneylender and Spice Merchant, require the broader audit in #429. The new menus and Courtier count follow that predicate; their Capitalism regression tests cover an immediate-coin Militia. They do not certify every Capitalism interaction. The fixed study boards contain no Capitalism project.

Trader exchange: the reaction is now owner-ordered after the original gain. Original gain identity, on-gain abilities and Exile/Gatekeeper accounting remain attached to that card; the received Silver goes to discard and is not gained. Exchanges obey physical pile availability and stop-moving rules. A strategy can override the reaction as well as its ordering. #430 is implemented in this PR, with nested Border Village, moved-card and off-turn physical regressions. Unsupported pile models and unrelated Trader tactics are not certified.

Intervals use conservative Hoeffding bounds on independent seed blocks, averaging the two seats before inference. They are per-comparison, without a multiple-comparison correction; normal score-margin intervals are secondary and appear in the raw data. The frozen selection rule requires every opponent's screen lower bound to exceed zero even after worst-case capped-outcome sensitivity. All fourteen selections retained the existing policy before validation.

Contextual gain rankings regress against Smithy money: Armory -29.0 points and Artificer -32.5 points. This is evidence against installing those particular alternatives on this panel, not evidence that no stronger policy exists. For Stables, Scheme and Moneylender, all 600 matched validation records per card have identical outcomes and decision counters across the two policies; this purchase panel does not distinguish their alternatives. Knight and Rogue contrasts also have many unchanged records. These rows establish no tactical advantage and need other board/hand panels before drawing stronger conclusions.

The 150-turn cap affected 10 screening games and 24 validation games. Each raw comparison records counts, completed outcomes and worst-case delta ranges, plus confidence bounds widened by capped-game rates. Capped games are not silently excluded. Knight/Rogue victim callbacks fire only against opponents that use those attacks; zero exposure against money/engine opponents is not tactical coverage of the victim choice. All endgame and absence scenarios remain visible, including Moneylender's constrained hand without Copper.

Review follow-up and evidence boundaries

Exact-head and bounded static reviews identified Duration targeting, the initial Warlord hand-play gate and preservation of Trader’s original gain event. Counterfeit now excludes Duration targets and checks the initial hand play before moving the card; a permitted second copy may still replay legally. Trader resolves as a current post-gain exchange. New physical tests cover Merchant Ship, Search, Frigate, Gold, a Knight, a non-Supply Ghost and an unreturnable Zombie. The existing Lurker/Lich tests now model a real card removed from its pile before it is trashed, then check its return.

The saved strategy studies predate these review corrections. They retain their original fingerprints and outcomes; they were not rerun or regraded. None of their declared boards contains Trader or Capitalism, so these physical cases are not strength evidence from those studies. Current correction validation is synthetic regression coverage and the full test suite, not a new strategy experiment.

Follow-up caller audit: Groom, Replace and Wild Hunt now use original-gain observations for their conditional bonuses; a received exchange card cannot replace that identity. The existing Hoard purchase helper classifies the bought Victory card rather than the received Silver, without claiming a wider Hoard rules audit. Rebuild’s pile-restoration fallback now checks the original physical return marker, so a nested Duplicate cannot cause a second return. Hill Fort checks source-zone availability; Black Market passes the result to handlers. Those uses are distinguished from original-gain type/identity bonuses.

While fixing Groom’s type branches, the publisher Groom notes also confirmed its printed Victory bonus: draw one card and get one Action. That branch and its required non-Supply Horse setup are corrected in #431, with multi-type, exchange and Snowy Village regressions. General Groom menus/costs and tactical strength remain unreviewed.

Remaining work

Blockade gain movement: a watcher is armed only after the original gained card is physically set aside. Trader exchange and Watchtower movement leave no new watcher; a failed later replay preserves an earlier stored card and its immunity snapshot. A stored card can trigger from a virtual Supply play, but only on the gaining opponent’s own turn, and stops when returned. These synthetic regressions implement #432 using the publisher Seaside rules. Two successful plays still overwrite the single storage slot; that separately reproduced conservation defect remains open in #433. Gain-choice forwarding and tactical preference strength remain unreviewed. Saved strategy studies predate this correction.

Initial storage and placement: #434 gives Blockade an owned initial set-aside destination before gain effects, rather than recovering a discard afterward. Nomad Camp’s deck default and Ghost Town, Guardian, Night Watchman and reduced-cost Den of Sin’s hand defaults yield to that source destination. Their printed defaults now determine initial placement for ordinary gains too, so reactions do not precede placement or resurrect a moved card. Night Watchman’s inherited immediate-play-on-gain hook was inconsistent with its printed gain-to-hand instruction; it is replaced by initial hand placement, without a full Night Watchman audit. Villa’s actual on-gain move can leave Blockade storage, as can Trader and Watchtower. Source ownership, stop-moving after shuffles and exchanges, subsequent own-turn attacks and next-turn returns have synthetic coverage. These review corrections do not add strategy-strength evidence or change the saved studies; the five placement cards’ other choices and interactions remain unreviewed.

The inventory still has 45 scoped reviewed entries and 565 explicitly unreviewed registered names. Trader, Groom and Blockade are additional scoped rules entries outside the fourteen-policy benchmark, with no isolated tactical-strength claim. Those entries do not certify every interaction of their cards. The broad parent stays open: migrate Inheritance, complete Capitalism text classification and fix successful Blockade replay storage, continue expansion/active-board audits, and investigate better contextual policies with new screening/validation evidence. No optimizer parameters are added here; callable overrides and optimizer-search coverage remain separate.

Historical studies, saved tournament standings and card ranks remain preserved. This report adds evidence under the corrected engine; it does not silently regrade earlier results.
