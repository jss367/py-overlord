Barge, Sleigh and Torturer: Timing and Reaction Policy Evaluation

Choose when resources become useful, spend reactions deliberately, and keep attack choices separate from their mandatory costs.

Measured defaults, with room for strategy choices. Barge and Sleigh now expose their dedicated decisions to ordinary strategies. Fresh-seed validation improves the three tested Barge engines by 6.5–16 percentage points. A first Torturer response protected purchases too aggressively and accumulated costly Curses; that experiment remains an opt-in strategy choice. The final default keeps the earlier Copper tolerance. Sleigh gains are mostly small or uncertain, and two final games reach the turn cap.

When the shared defaults act

  • Barge: take resources now when there are remaining Actions or Villagers and drawable cards. Without an Action, compare printed usable Treasure income to next-turn Action resources with a 20% future discount; an extra Buy has a modest value when the current hand has at least $6. Defer when the play phase has passed or no cards can be drawn. Prefer now before a likely last Province/Colony purchase and for immediate Chameleon money. The estimate uses deck/discard composition, never hidden draw order.
  • Sleigh: put a useful gain in hand while its Action, Treasure or Night phase is available; otherwise put it on the deck. Decline junk, pure points, already moved gains and gains already in the useful destination. Retain a last playable Sleigh's two Horses rather than spending it for Silver. This is a bounded cost estimate, not an interpreter of every card ability.
  • Torturer: take the empty-pile Curse option for free. With Curses available, prefer discarding dead points, Curses, Copper and terminal Actions beyond the next hand's printed Action capacity. Discard selection protects printed economy, useful villages and live hybrids. The baseline retains the previous willingness to discard Copper instead of polluting the deck.

Special card text, repeated attacks, landscapes, discretionary resource spending and three-pile endings can change the best choice. A strategy override remains the appropriate place for those plans.

Strategy contracts and rules audit

should_resolve_barge_now(state, player) returns a boolean; absent or invalid answers use the shared fallback. choose_sleigh_reaction(state, player, gained_card) returns "hand", "deck" or None. An explicit decline or invalid reaction answer keeps the Sleigh in hand. Legal reactions discard that physical copy exactly once.

Torturer already forwarded choose_torturer_response(state, player) (true means discard) and choose_cards_to_discard(..., reason="torturer"). Both hooks receive the responder, while the attacker remains the engine's current player. Neither requires a duplicate hook. Once discard is chosen, invalid, duplicate or short physical selections are filled to two cards, or all available cards. The selected batch leaves hand before discard reactions resolve.

The publisher's Intrigue rules permit either Torturer response even with no Curses or no cards in hand, and resolve opponents starting left of the attacker. The publisher's Menagerie rules require a timing choice on every Barge play and prevent Sleigh from moving a gain after another effect has moved it.

  • Torturer legality, discards and turn order (#406): choices are honored without forcing the other response; mandatory discards are completed.
  • Repeated deferred Barge payloads (#407): each queued play supplies its own three cards and Buy. Actual Throne Room plays retain the Barge and multiplier through the delayed turn, including mixed timing choices.
  • Curse destination and gain reactions (#408): declare a gain to hand before resolving reactions, preserving Watchtower's trash/topdeck and avoiding card resurrection.
  • Sleigh gain movement (#409): only move a gain still in its recorded original destination. A strategy can discard Sleigh voluntarily after another mover, but that cannot move the gain again.

The fixes above are included here. General simultaneous gain ordering remains in a separate rules issue (#410): the engine offers Sleigh before Sheepdog and stops after an accepted Sleigh. Tests certify that existing path, independent copies, declines, off-turn use and Watchtower interactions. They do not certify every legal ordering, movement away and back to the same zone, Command proxy, or multiplier/Way combination. Chameleon immediate and delayed Barge behavior is explicitly tested.

Exploratory response and fresh validation

The first study ran 9,600 games using seeds 393000–393099 on the diagnostic kingdom and 403000–403099 on the registered boards. It tested a Torturer response that would discard Copper only when the remaining money preserved an attainable Supply purchase. Against Torturer money, Barge money lost 10.5 percentage points (interval −16.78 to −4.22), and Torturer money lost 17.5 points (−23.31 to −11.69). Their score margins fell 2.74 and 3.43 points. This study also found a small −0.29-point margin change for Selective Envoy Collection against Smithy Money, with interval −0.5762 to −0.0038. There were no capped games in that study.

Those original per-seed outcomes are preserved. The purchase-preserving response remains available as tactical_defaults.torturer_should_discard_preserving_buy, assignable to a strategy's existing response hook. The final default was selected before testing on fresh seeds. The evaluator's exploratory flag also restores the earlier opponent response; a reproduction check matched all 400 game outcomes and decision counts in the Torturer-money mirror comparison.

Regressions, caps and practical limits

All final observed win-share changes are nonnegative in this panel. Several are uncertain, and the two Envoy engines retain small negative score-margin point estimates with intervals spanning zero. The three existing Barge engines improve against both opponents. The Torturer-money mirror gains 6.5 percentage points (2.4 to 10.6). This does not establish optimal default play or justify extrapolating to other kingdoms.

Two final games reached the 150-turn cap: the Fisherman/Infirmary engine in seat 1 at seed 403207 against each opponent. The recorded result is its score at cutoff, not a completed-game win. The table retains those outcomes and flags them. Removing the entire affected seed block from each comparison gives this sensitivity check:

  • Black Cat and Livery: Fisherman and Infirmary Engine against Big Money: removing the entire affected seed block leaves 99 pairs, a +12.63 percentage-point change, with interval [+5.40, +19.85].
  • Black Cat and Livery: Fisherman and Infirmary Engine against Smithy Money: removing the entire affected seed block leaves 99 pairs, a +15.66 percentage-point change, with interval [+8.13, +23.19].

The six registered Barge-engine comparisons include those two cap-sensitive rows.
