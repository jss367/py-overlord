First Game

Skip Smithy. Buy Militia, then Markets, then money. On the base-set "First Game" kingdom (Cellar, Market, Merchant, Militia, Mine, Moat, Remodel, Smithy, Village, Workshop) a money deck with up to three Militias and three Markets beat the community answer, Smithy money with one Militia, in 71.7% of 1,000 paired games, and beat straight Big Money in 89.4%.

Every engine, Remodel, Merchant, Mine, Workshop and Cellar variant tried lost to the money line. Two players, Estate starts, no Colonies or Shelters.

The strategy

The buy list is plain Big Money with two kingdom cards added, in this order:

  1. Province; Duchy once four or fewer Provinces remain; Estate once two or fewer remain.
  2. Gold.
  3. Market, up to three copies.
  4. Militia, up to three copies.
  5. Silver.

Play Markets first, then one Militia. There is no Smithy, no Village and no trashing. Militia is the only terminal, so up to three copies collide rarely and the attack lands most turns from the mid-game on. Markets are cantrips that add a coin, a buy and no terminal risk, which is exactly what a Militia deck wants: more coin density without more dead draws under the mirror attack.

The board's cards that do not appear: Smithy (its draw gets cut to three cards by the opponent's Militia and it collides with Militia), Cellar, Merchant, Mine, Moat, Remodel, Village and Workshop.

How the search ran

The ten cards were audited against their printed text first (the repository's standing lesson from the Hyderabad, Vientiane and Port Moresby boards). All ten base-set implementations matched, including Merchant's once-per-turn Silver bonus, Militia's discard-to-three and Remodel's mandatory trash.

The search itself was a hand-variant round robin: one parameterised money/engine strategy family (scripts/search_first_game.py) with caps and gates as parameters, played in paired seats so each shuffle seed is used from both starting positions. Ties count half.

What lost and why

  • Smithy money. The classic answer for this kingdom, but under a Militia attack a Smithy hand of three cards draws into an average of about four coins, and Smithy collides with the Militia the deck also wants. Removing both Smithies from the known-best encoding raised its average win rate by 11 points in stage 1; adding a Smithy to the Militia/Market line cost 10–20 points in stages 2 and 3.
  • Village/Smithy/Market engines. Three shapes were tried (two to four Villages paired to Smithies, with Markets, with and without Remodel). All finished at 25–33% average: no +Buy beyond Market, no trashing worth the tempo, and Militia hits the engine's five-card hands hardest.
  • Remodel. Estate-to-Silver early and Gold-to-Province late, played only when such a target is in hand, still cost 12 points. A terminal that competes with Militia for the action and gives up a Gold is not worth it in a game that ends around turn 16.
  • Merchant. A cantrip that only pays with a Silver in the same hand; it dilutes the money faster than it adds. Cost 16 points in stage 1 and 5 points in stage 3.
  • Mine, Workshop, Cellar. Mine's Silver-to-Gold upgrade is too slow; Workshop gains nothing this deck wants; Cellar was neutral to slightly negative. Moat was neutral to slightly positive as the fourth card, blocking about half of the mirror's attacks for a $2 card.
  • Greening gates. Duchy at three Provinces left lost 7 points; at five left, neutral. The standard four-Province gate was kept.
  • Market over Gold. Taking Market ahead of Gold at $6+ cost 9 points; Markets are a supplement to the money, not the economy.

Genetic search

The island genetic search (scripts/island_evolve.py, six islands, population 20, 15 generations, 12 games per evaluation, default Big Money and library panel) was run on the board as an independent check. Every island converged to a money deck; none kept a Village. The island seeded with the Village/Smithy engine archetype converged to Militia ×3 and Market ×4 over money, the same shape the hand search found, and the island seeded with the known-best Smithy/Militia strategy dropped its Smithies and kept a single Militia.

The genetic search found the right cards but not the right ordering: its best champion takes Militia ahead of Gold and greens from six Provinces left, and loses 61.5% to the hand-tuned list. The earlier calibration-suite run on this board (population 30, 30 generations) had its champion tie the known-best at 51.5%, which is consistent with a search that stops at Militia money without the Markets.

Reproduction

From the repository root:

PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_first_game.py --stage 1
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_first_game.py --stage 2
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_first_game.py --stage 3
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_first_game.py --stage 4   # 1,000 games per pair

PYTHONPATH=. python -m dominion.simulation.strategy_battle \
    "First Game Militia Market" "First Game Smithy Militia" \
    --board boards/calibration/first_game.txt --games 400 --no-report --print

Stage seeds are fixed (1000, 2000, 3000, 4000), so the tables above reproduce exactly. Pass --checkpoint <file> to save finished matchups and resume after an interruption, and --workers N to set the process count.
