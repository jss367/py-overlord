Search date: September 8, 2026. Two-player games throughout.

Card audit

All ten implementations were read against the printed card text and then exercised in a scripted game state (King's Court on Wharf and on Bridge, the Peasant exchange at clean-up, Cultist chains and the Ruins pile, Disciple, Teacher's token, Masquerade passing, Wandering Minstrel's sift, Steward's modes). Unlike earlier boards the rules were essentially right. Three edge defects were fixed, with tests:

  1. Remake compared printed costs. "Costing exactly $1 more" uses current costs, so with a Bridge in play a trashed Copper (cost $0) must become a $2 card, and under three Bridges a $4 card. The simulator found nothing to gain. It now uses the modified cost for both the trashed card and the candidates, and excludes Debt and Potion costs.
  2. King's Court forced a target. The card says "you may"; the simulator played the first Action in hand whenever the AI declined, which made King's Court triple a Chapel. Declining is now respected.
  3. Remake's mandatory gain fell back to the first pile (an Estate) when the strategy wanted none of the options. It now takes the least harmful option, an Action or Treasure over a Victory card.

Search infrastructure was extended alongside: GeneticAI now forwards the Steward mode, Masquerade pass, Cultist chain, Traveller exchange, Teacher token and discard decisions to the strategy; the card capability table marks Wharf as draw and Masquerade, Steward and Remake as trashers so the engine enumerator sees them; three PriorityRule conditions were added (treasure_value_in_deck, deck_group_diff, not_); and exported island champions keep their hand-written seed class as a base so decision hooks survive evolution.

Board texture

  • Four trashers (Chapel, Masquerade, Steward, Remake) make a thin deck cheap. Masquerade is the one that draws while it thins, and it hands junk to the opponent.
  • Wandering Minstrel is the only village, and a cantrip one: +1 Card +2 Actions, then it puts the Actions among the top three cards back and discards the rest, so a thin engine deck keeps drawing live cards. Ruins are Actions, so a Cultist victim's Minstrel keeps Ruins on top.
  • Wharf is the draw and the Buys: +2 Cards +1 Buy this turn and next. King's Court on Wharf is six cards and three Buys, twice.
  • Bridge is the payload: +$1 +1 Buy and every card costs $1 less, stacking. King's Court on Bridge makes Provinces cost $5 and adds three Buys; with a few plain Bridges on top the deck buys several Provinces a turn.
  • Cultist is the only attack: +2 Cards, chains itself, and hands out Ruins. Against a Masquerade deck the Ruins come straight back.
  • Peasant is a $2 +Buy/+$1 that walks to Soldier, Fugitive, Disciple (a Throne Room that also gains a copy) and Teacher. It is slower than the Bridge and Wharf Buys the engine already has.

Recommendation

The best strategy found is published as Albuquerque Best Found, defined in generated_strategies/albuquerque_best_found.py (strategy page). It is the Masquerade Bridge Engine seed with the two hand-search adjustments that measured best.

Best foundMasquerade into Minstrel / Wharf / Bridge / King's Court

Open Masquerade (turn 1 to 4). Then, in order of what $ buys: Province once a King's Court and three payload cards are owned (or from turn 12, or with five Provinces left); King's Court up to two while two payload cards are owned; Wharf up to two; Wandering Minstrel while terminals exceed Minstrels by more than one; Bridge up to four; Minstrel up to four; one Silver (a second while the deck is short of money before turn 8); Duchy with two Provinces left and Estates with spare Buys. Trash Curses, Ruins, Estates while more than two Provinces remain, and Coppers only while the deck keeps more than $6 of Treasure (more than $3 once three Bridges are owned). Play Minstrel first, King's Court when it has a target, then Wharf, Masquerade, Bridge. King's Court triples another King's Court with four Actions in hand, else Bridge, else Wharf. With two piles empty and the lead, drain the smallest pile.

Why this strategy wins

  • It finishes in 13 to 15 turns. Traced games against Cultist Wharf money end with six to eight Provinces for the engine on turn 13 or 14; money has three or four by then.
  • Masquerade beats Chapel here. Chapel thins faster but costs a turn each time it fires and leaves the deck too poor to buy the engine; Masquerade draws two, thins one card a turn, and passes the Cultist player's Ruins back. The Chapel version of the same core lost 31% head to head in the seed round robin.
  • Two King's Courts, four Bridges. The seed wanted three and six. Hand search found the smaller counts better: the deck reaches its greening threshold sooner and the mega-turn still buys three or four Provinces.
  • The greening gate matters. An unconditional Province rule greened on turn 9 with two Bridges and lost to money; waiting for a King's Court plus three payload cards (or turn 12) was worth about 20 points.

Method

  1. Card audit (above), then ten hand-written seeds covering four money lines (Double Cultist, Wharf, Cultist Wharf, Masquerade) and six engine theories (Chapel, Masquerade, Steward, Remake and Cultist openers on a Bridge core, a Wharf-and-Gold engine, and a Traveller line).
  2. Seed round robin (100 games per pair, both seats, shared shuffles). The first engine seeds lost to every money seed; traces showed four play-skill defects, fixed in the seed vocabulary: trashing rules that counted engine cards as economy and stripped every Copper, an unconditional Province rule, a village rule that only compared Minstrels to Bridges, and King's Court choosing its target from the main play order (it tripled Chapel). After the fixes the Masquerade Bridge Engine led the round robin.
  3. Hand local search around the leader over the engine's counts (King's Courts, Bridges, Wharves, Minstrels, Silvers, greening turn, Duchy timing, opener) against three fixed opponents, 100 then 200 games per match.
  4. Island evolution: nine islands (seven engine seeds and two money seeds), population 20, 10 generations, 16 games per evaluation, panel of the two leading engines and two money seeds.
  5. Cross-island tournament between the champions, the hand leader and the best seeds, then a long confirmation of the winner.

Search notes

  • The board's ranking was hidden by play skill, not rules. On earlier boards the pre-search audit flipped rankings by fixing cards. Here the cards were right and the first engines still lost by 20 to 40 points to money. Each of the four seed defects (Copper trashing without an economy floor, greening on the first $8, villages counted against one terminal, King's Court targeting through the main play order) was found by tracing a single game, and each was worth 10 to 20 points.
  • Money is genuinely good here. Wharf money and Cultist Wharf money beat Big Money 98% to 99% and are a real bar; Masquerade money was the strongest pure money line before the engines were fixed.
  • Turn-limit stalls. One game in a hundred against Big Money ran to the turn limit: the engine emptied the Estate and Duchy piles with its spare Buys and was then too green to afford the last Province, while Big Money's endgame guard refused it from behind. The engine base class now drains the smallest pile when two piles are empty and it leads.
  • Steward and Remake openers underperformed. Steward's forced "trash two" fires only when two hand cards pass the trash list; Remake's mandatory gain turns Coppers into $2 cards under Bridge, so the seed refuses to Remake while a Bridge is in play. Neither opener matched Masquerade's draw.
  • Peasant and Cultist did not earn a slot. The Traveller line is too slow for a 14-turn game, and a Cultist in the engine cost about 15 points (a terminal that draws two is worse than a Wharf here, and its Ruins come back through Masquerade).
