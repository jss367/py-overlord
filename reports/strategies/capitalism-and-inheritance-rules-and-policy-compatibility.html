Capitalism and Inheritance: Current Rules and Policy Compatibility

Use printed coin instructions for Capitalism and keep Estate separate from the Action it plays through Inheritance. The corrected engine retains existing strategy preferences. A new compatibility panel exercises those preferences under both engine versions; it does not select a stronger policy.

Current rules

Capitalism: 485 registered printed Action names have explicit classifications: 158 contain qualifying printed plus-coin text and 327 do not. Choices, conditional bonuses, deferred instructions and Duration coins qualify, including Courtier, Minion, Moneylender, Spice Merchant, Merchant, Harbor Village, Sailor and Secret Cave. Coffers, cost reductions and cost limits without a printed plus-coin amount do not qualify. Teacher itself qualifies because it references its plus-coin token; putting that token on Smithy does not change Smithy's printed text. See the official online release explanation. Changing executable stats does not change the classification. Emporium qualifies despite its legacy missing coin; Encampment, Goatherd and Marauder do not qualify despite legacy positive coin stats. Their underlying printed bonuses, types and other instructions need separate card corrections. The actual turn owner's project changes types everywhere, including opponents' cards during reactions. Legal plays, gains, reveals, discards, trashes and Command menus use shared live type predicates. Storyteller also receives its previously missing initial coin and plays its Treasures through shared handling. See the Renaissance publisher rules, page 8, classification maintenance notes and issue #429.

Inheritance: the event sets aside a physical non-Command Action from an exposed Supply pile, using current cost and respecting Potion/Debt restrictions. Reserve, Duration and Victory Actions are eligible. This is not a gain; the card is owned and included in scoring. On the owner's turns, Estates everywhere are Action–Command–Victory. An Estate retains its name, printed points and physical identity; it plays the owner's set-aside target as a separate Action play. It gains neither the target's types nor its Capitalism qualification. Both plays have their own counters, Ways, pile-token bonuses and observers. Pending Flagship and Daimyo replays skip the Command Estate and replay its non-Command target through shared play handling; the physical replay sources retain the appropriate Duration dependencies. Daimyo charges expire on the actual turn that created them, including off-turn plays. A source outside play cannot move itself to the Tavern mat, deck or trash, or supply a while-in-play discount. Each Estate play retains that Estate only for its own unfinished instructions, including Durations played later by Mastermind or Royal Galley. Unused Cargo Ship allowances do not retain another Estate or reactivate on a later turn. Copied games preserve those ownership links. See the Adventures publisher rules, pages 11–12 and issue #427.

Cargo Ship now keeps a separate rest-of-turn gain allowance for each play and returns all stored cards once. Unused allowances expire with the turn. Church keeps each play's storage batch and next-turn trash opportunity, including plays that set aside zero cards, consistent with the publisher's Church notes. Archive also preserves independent sets of cards for repeated plays and renews only batches that remain unfinished, following the Empires publisher notes. An inherited Garrison remains token-free: the publisher's Allies notes, page 7, allow tokens on Garrison only while it is in play. Its immediate coins resolve, but the permanently set-aside card does not schedule a token draw. These fixes cover the repeated physical sources exposed by Inheritance.

Physical regression coverage

Tests cover all registered classifications, conditional and delayed Buy-phase plays, actual turn ownership during reactions, Courtier type counting, three Treasure menus, Bandit trashing an opponent's conditional coin Action, Guildhall gains, Command exclusions, live costs, separate Estate/source names and token bonuses, Citadel replays, Ways, source scoring, Reserve movement failures, nested multipliers, repeated Durations, and Gear, Cargo Ship and Church storage conservation. The source remains separately owned through cleanup and delayed returns.

The classification pass does not certify every instruction or tactical choice of all 485 Actions. The decision inventory has 57 scoped entries and 553 explicitly unreviewed names among 610 registered names. Landing Party still needs a separate rules correction: its printed +2 Cards/+2 Actions and first-Treasure-play return timing are not implemented. This change protects a permanently set-aside source from its existing gain handler; it does not certify that handler's trigger. See the Plunder publisher rules, page 5. Individual card implementations and unmeasured policy contexts remain part of the broader card-decision audit.

Matched policy compatibility panel

The new runner plays 720 games per engine: six fixed named baseline policies, two starting modifier configurations, three representative opponents, ten independent seeds per comparison, and both seats. Both players receive the same declared project and, where specified, Inheritance targeting Merchant before turn one. Estate is prioritized in Action play and removed from explicit Estate trash priorities for the Inheritance fixtures. These artificial starts isolate compatibility; they do not recommend when to purchase either landscape. The board contains Courtier, Stables, Spice Merchant, Counterfeit, Moneylender, Minion, Smithy, Village, Merchant and Militia.

There are 8199 recorded Estate selections on the earlier engine and 7585 on the corrected engine. Frozen policies are reevaluated without changing the original named-card screen, validation, registered-strategy studies or saved tournament results. Before/after outcome differences reflect engine changes and diverging random streams, not a comparison between new candidate policies.
