Three kingdoms from previously unused cards · September 17, 2026

Old Witch and Rabble

Ten previously unused kingdom piles, a reproducible strategy search, and a practical plan for the strongest policy found.

The kingdom

Old Witch$5
Rabble$5
Recruiter$5
Silk Merchant$4
Spices$5
Squire$2
Caravan$4
Warehouse$3
Haggler$5
Soothsayer$5

Two players. Standard supply and starting decks: seven Coppers and three Estates each. No landscapes, Shelters, Colony, Platinum or Potion. The three kingdoms share no kingdom cards.

Old Witch combines draw with Curses, while Rabble attacks the opponent's next draws. Recruiter can turn Estates or Silk Merchants into Villagers, and Spices provides money, a buy and Coffers on gain. Squire and Caravan offer a more conventional engine; Soothsayer offers an alternative curse-and-Gold plan.

The overall tournament winner

1 Warehouse, 2 Soothsayer, 1 Caravan, 5 Spices. This policy scored 69.0% in the eight-policy finalist tournament and 94.9% against treasure-only money on 1,000 fresh validation games. Ties count as half a win.

Matchup caveat: its toughest tested finalist matchup was 1 Old Witch, 2 Haggler, 5 Spices, 1 Squire, against which it scored 46.2% (approximate 95% interval 43.2–49.2%). The recommendation optimizes the overall tournament result; opponent-specific counters and close alternatives matter.

Opening preference: Silver. During your first two turns, prefer that card when affordable and not already owned; otherwise follow the purchase priorities below. This specifies a preference, not a guaranteed opening split.

  1. Buy Province from your turn 8, or whenever four or fewer Provinces remain.
  2. Buy Duchy at 2 or fewer Provinces remaining, and Estate at one or fewer. These scoring buys precede deck-building targets.
  3. Next take the first affordable card below whose ownership target has not been met, in the listed order. Cards lower on the list can be bought before a more expensive target when money is short.
    1. Warehouse: up to 1 owned at a time.
    2. Soothsayer: up to 2 owned at a time.
    3. Caravan: up to 1 owned at a time.
    4. Spices: up to 5 owned at a time.
  4. Then buy Gold up to 99 copies and Silver up to 99. A cap of 99 is effectively uncapped. Pass if nothing on the list is affordable; do not add early Estates or extra Copper.

Play Caravan before Warehouse, discard Victory cards, Curses and then Copper with Warehouse, and finish with Soothsayer. Soothsayer supplies Gold and curses the opponent; Spices supplies $2 and another buy, plus two Coffers when gained. Use the extra buying power for Provinces from turn eight, or earlier when four or fewer remain. The simulator automatically uses Coffers to cover a purchase when needed.

The recommendation skips Old Witch and Rabble. Soothsayer offers a different attack plan: accumulate Gold while distributing Curses, then use Warehouse to filter weak hands. These are plausible reasons for the complete policy's result, rather than measured single-card effects.

What the search establishes

154 candidate policies were screened against four fixed opponents, with 40 games per pairing. Candidates included one-, two- and five-copy plans for every pile, money baselines, seeded engines and randomized mixtures. The top eight advanced to a complete round robin: 240 games per pairing, 1,680 per entrant. The tournament winner was frozen, then faced four finalists and two baselines for 1,000 games each on disjoint fresh seeds.

Every random seed is played in both seats. Scores include the equal-score, fewer-turns tiebreaker. In total, this board contributed 37,360 games: 24,640 screening, 6,720 tournament and 6,000 validation. 0 games reached the safeguard without a normal ending. All displayed rates include ties as half a win.

Intervals use the standard error of the 500 two-seat pair scores, preserving dependence within each seeded pair. They describe simulation uncertainty for a fixed matchup and are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Despite the highest overall tournament score, the recommendation scored only 46.2% against 1 Old Witch, 2 Haggler, 5 Spices, 1 Squire; its interval lies below 50%. That opponent is a demonstrated counter in this matchup. Consider its exact policy above when facing the recommended deck.

Scope: this is the best policy found within the tested purchase plans and decision rules, not a proof of optimal Dominion play. The search varies ownership caps, purchase order, opening preference, treasure density, greening, and two play settings. It does not exhaust tactical policies, tune every card-specific choice, or model human opponents. Results use this repository's simulator, including its fixed card heuristics; they are not an independent certification of all card rules. Finalists differ in multiple settings, so their ranking does not isolate the value of a single card.

Rules correction before the recorded search: Soothsayer previously put the opponent's Curse directly into their hand. It now gains to the discard pile and then draws a card, following the official Guilds rulebook (archived copy). Regression tests cover the gain destination and the absence of a draw when Curses run out. All results shown here were regenerated after that correction; the earlier exploratory results were discarded.

Why these cards qualify

Before writing these strategies, all 30 selected kingdom cards had zero references in the catalog of 135 registered strategies. An additional syntax-tree scan found no exact card-name string literals in the existing Python strategy modules, including unpublished variants. Basic supply and starting cards are exempt. This is a repository-specific definition: it does not claim these cards are unused in Dominion generally, and indirect or dynamically constructed references can escape a static audit.

The baseline revision is 7d1b4b1b9aa0874e51362c4201cbebd83dfbd21d. The selection audit preserves the original candidate pool and all three boards. Newly published strategies will now make their chosen cards appear as used.
