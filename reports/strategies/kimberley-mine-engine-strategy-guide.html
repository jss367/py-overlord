Board built for Mine

Kimberley

A two-player Colony board where Mine is the best card. Mine trashes a Treasure and gains one costing up to $3 more directly into hand, so one play turns Gold into Platinum you spend the same turn. Kimberley surrounds it with cards that pay out on every trash: Sewers trashes a second card, Tomb awards a point, Priest adds $2, and Market Square turns into a Gold.

The published engine won 87.0% of confirmation games against the strongest plan the search found that never buys Mine, and 97.3% against Colony Big Money with Smithy.

The board

Kingdom: Mine, Throne Room, King's Court, Laboratory, Smithy, Mining Village, Market Square, Priest, Hoard, Bank. Colony and Platinum are in the supply. Landscapes: the Sewers project and the Tomb landmark. Board file: boards/kimberley.txt. The multipliers are on the board because Mine is the card they most want to repeat; the search found the engine is stronger without them, and the ablation table below shows by how much.

PieceWhat it does for Mine
Platinum and ColonyGives Mine a top rung. Gold costs $6, so Mine reaches Platinum ($9) in one step, and Colonies keep the game long enough for the climb to matter.
SewersEvery Mine play trashes a second card from hand. The engine removes Estates and spare Coppers without a dedicated trasher.
Tomb+1 VP per trash. With Sewers doubling trashes, the published engine banks about 13 Tomb points per game.
Priest+$2 per later trash this turn. Played before Mine, it turns each climb into money as well.
Market SquareReacts to any trash by becoming a Gold, which Mine later turns into Platinum. Its +Buy spends the surplus.
Throne Room, King's CourtRepeat Mine: Copper to Silver to Gold, or Gold to Platinum twice. Tempting, but neither helped in testing: King's Court at $7 competes with Platinum, and Throne Room at $4 competes with Priest and Sewers while needing Mine in the same hand.
Laboratory, Smithy, Mining VillageDraw and Actions so Mine and Priest can both fire. Mining Village trashing itself also triggers Sewers and Tomb.
Hoard, BankAlternative Mine targets from Silver and Gold. Neither beat plain Platinum climbing in the search.

How to play it

  1. Open Mine/Priest. On $5/$2 buy Mine and nothing; on $4/$3 buy Priest and Silver or Market Square. Opening Laboratory instead of Mine was within noise; opening Silver instead of Priest lost clearly.
  2. One Mine is enough. A second Mine lost 55.6% of games to the single-Mine engine. Play Mine every turn it can climb: trash Gold when Platinum remains in the supply, otherwise Silver, otherwise Copper. Never trash Platinum. Upgrading Copper first instead lost 69.7%.
  3. Buy Sewers with your first spare $3. It converts every Mine play into two trashes. Let it eat Estates first, then Coppers while you own more than three. Skipping Sewers, skipping Priest, and opening Silver instead of Priest were the three costliest changes tested after skipping Mine.
  4. Priest before Mine when you have the Actions. Mining Village or Laboratory gives the second Action. Priest trashes an Estate or Copper, then Mine's trash and the Sewers trash each add $2. Skipping Priest lost 75.9% of games to the published engine. Market Square was close to neutral on its own; it is there for the Gold reaction and the +Buy.
  5. Skip Throne Room and King's Court. The engine plays one Mine per turn and wants every other purchase to be Platinum, draw, or a trash trigger. Adding one Throne Room lost 61.4% to the published engine; adding one King's Court was a coin flip. If you do own one, point it at Mine.
  6. Money: Platinum at $9, one Gold, one Silver. Mine manufactures the rest. Add two Laboratories, two Market Squares, and a Mining Village once you own two terminals.
  7. Green late. Buy Colony once you own a Platinum, Province once four or fewer Colonies remain, Duchy at two. Tomb points arrive on their own; the engine scores about 13 of them a game.

Key rules: Mine gains the new Treasure to your hand, so it is playable this turn. Sewers triggers on each trash, including Priest's own trash and Mining Village trashing itself. Priest's +$2 applies only to trashes after its own. See the Mine, Sewers, and Priest rules.

What the simulations established

The search played 100,920 games: a 340-candidate screen at 32 games each against Colony Big Money with Smithy, a 40-candidate recheck at 400 games, a separate 120-candidate search for the best plan that never buys Mine (with an eight-candidate recheck), a first confirmation of the recheck leader, and a final confirmation of the published engine after the first confirmation's ablations showed it was stronger without Throne Room. Seats alternate; each shuffle seed is used in both seats; equal scores use the fewer-turns tiebreak and complete ties count as half a win. No game reached the turn limit.

The best no-Mine plan the search found (Priest opening, three Laboratories, King's Court with Smithy, three Market Squares, Hoard and Bank) won 67.9% against Colony Big Money with Smithy. Final confirmation of the published engine, 1,000 games per opponent:

Approximate sampling uncertainty for a 1,000-game result near 50% is three percentage points, before allowing for dependence between paired games. Changes whose paired 95% interval includes 50% (no Market Square, adding King's Court, the Laboratory opening) are not separated from the published engine; the second Mine's interval (52.9% to 58.3%) sits just above it.

Practical takeaway: Mine is the engine here, not a support card. The same policy family without Mine falls from 87.0% to 42.1% against the best no-Mine plan, and Sewers and Priest are the pieces that make the difference: Sewers turns each Mine play into a second trash and a second Tomb point, and Priest turns both trashes into money. The multipliers that seem made for Mine are the trap.

Reproduction

The registered strategy implementation is named Kimberley Mine Engine. Its decision hooks choose the Mine trash and gain, the multiplier target when one is owned, and the buy order; the static gain list records referenced cards and is not a literal purchase order. Class parameters preserve the alternatives used in the search.

PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_kimberley.py screen
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_kimberley.py recheck
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_kimberley.py nomine
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_kimberley.py confirm
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_kimberley.py final

The script uses six worker processes. Raw results are in the screen, the recheck, the no-Mine search, the first confirmation, and the final confirmation.

Corrections made before these results were recorded:

  • Sewers reads "when you trash a card other than with this, you may trash a card from your hand." The simulator let the Sewers trash re-trigger Sewers, so one trash could chain through every Estate and Copper in hand. It now yields at most one extra trash per trash, while Tomb, Priest, and Market Square still see both. Regression tests are in tests/test_sewers_project.py. The whole search was rerun after this fix; the earlier run had overstated the Sewers ablation. See the Sewers rules.
  • The Colony Big Money baselines with Hoard and Bank originally ranked Gold above their support card, so they never bought it. They now buy Hoard or Bank ahead of Gold until the cap.

The implementations of Mine, Throne Room, King's Court, Priest, Mining Village, Market Square, Hoard, Bank, and Tomb were also read against the card rules. Mine gains to hand and limits the gain to $3 above the trashed Treasure's cost; Tomb, Priest, Sewers, and Market Square all resolve from the shared trash hook.
