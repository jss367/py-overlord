Simulator fixes made before searching

The pre-search audit compared all twelve implementations (ten cards, the Rich trait, and Madman) with the printed card text, verified against the Dominion Strategy wiki card data. Six rules defects were found and fixed first, with tests in tests/test_bilbao_board_rules.py. Two of them change how the whole board plays:

  1. Shaman was a Duration card with a private trash rule. The simulator had Shaman stay in play and, only for the player who played it, gain a card from the trash on their next turn. The real card is a plain cantrip (+1 Action, +$1, you may trash a card from your hand), and its second half is a game-wide setup rule: "in games using this, at the start of your turn, gain a card from the trash costing up to $6". That applies to every player from turn one whether or not anyone owns a Shaman, and the gain is mandatory. So on this board every trashed Estate or Copper is handed to the next player, and a Fool's Gold trashed for its reaction comes straight back to its owner.
  2. Raider attacked every hand. The real card only makes players with five or more cards in hand discard a copy of a card you have in play.
  3. Hermit was trashed when it became a Madman. Current card text says "exchange this for a Madman": the Hermit goes back onto its pile. With Shaman in the game a trashed Hermit would have been recoverable from the trash.
  4. Grotto set aside the four cheapest cards with no choice. The real card lets the player choose up to four cards; it is now an AI hook (choose_cards_to_set_aside_for_grotto) whose default sets aside dead cards only, and a Grotto that sets nothing aside does not stay in play.
  5. Wheelwright discarded junk whenever any cheap Action existed, even if the strategy wanted none of them. The discard is optional, so the gain choice (the strategy's gain list) now decides whether to discard.
  6. Anvil discarded a Copper before knowing whether the strategy wanted any $4 gain; because the gain is mandatory after the discard, the fallback then grabbed a random $4 card. The gain is now chosen first and the discard is skipped when nothing is wanted.

New AI hooks, all overridable on a strategy and routed through GeneticAI to strategy methods where present: choose_card_to_gain_from_trash_with_shaman, choose_cards_to_set_aside_for_grotto, choose_wheelwright_discard, should_trash_fools_gold_for_gold (the reaction was a hard-coded heuristic), choose_anvil_treasure_to_discard, and Hermit's trash and gain choices, which now follow the strategy's trash and gain lists.

Board texture

  • There is no +Buy. Extra gains come only from Anvil (discard a Treasure to gain a card costing up to $4), Hermit (gain up to $3), Wheelwright (discard a card to gain an Action costing no more), the Rich trait and Shaman's setup rule.
  • Rich Fool's Gold is a Silver factory. Every Fool's Gold gained, bought or Anvil-gained, brings a Silver. The Fool's Golds pay $1 then $4 each; the Silvers pay Feodum.
  • Anvil is the board's economy. $1, and each play turns a Copper into "Fool's Gold + Silver" or a Feodum. Three Anvils mean roughly one extra gain per turn all game in a deck with one Buy.
  • Feodum is worth 1 VP per 3 Silvers; a deck that ends with 12-15 Silvers scores 4-5 VP per Feodum, for $4.
  • Shaman's setup rule makes trashing double-edged. A trashed card costing up to $6 must be taken by the next player. Against a deck that never trashes that is a junking attack; in a mirror the junk ping-pongs.
  • Hermit exchanges itself for a Madman on a turn with no Buy-phase gain; Madman doubles the hand. Wandering Minstrel is the only village, Sheepdog the only draw.
  • Raider is a $6 terminal that pays $3 next turn; its attack only touches five-card hands and only copies of cards in play.

Recommendation

The best strategy found is published as Bilbao Best Found, defined in generated_strategies/bilbao_best_found.py.

This is not a formal proof of optimal play. It is the best strategy found by the current simulator, hand-written seeds, focused local search around the leader, and island-model genetic evolution.

The shape is Anvil / Fool's Gold / Feodum money:

  • Buy Anvil with the first $3-$4 hands until you hold three (by turn 10).
  • Play Anvil before other Treasures and discard a Copper to gain a Feodum once the deck holds three Silvers, otherwise a Fool's Gold (which brings a Silver). Skip the gain only when the Copper is needed for $8.
  • Buy Province at $8, Duchy from six Provinces left, Gold only at $7, Feodum at $4-$6 once you have three Silvers, Fool's Gold ahead of Silver with anything else, never Estates.
  • Always react with Fool's Gold when the opponent gains a Province: the Gold goes on your deck and Shaman's rule returns the Fool's Gold at the start of your next turn.
  • Never trash anything (every trashed card is a gift to the opponent).

Why This Strategy Wins

  • Two free cards a turn. An Anvil in hand plus a Copper is a Fool's Gold and a Silver, on top of the buy. The deck reaches 12-15 Silvers and 5-8 Fool's Golds without ever spending a Buy on Silver.
  • Feodum is a $4 Province. With that many Silvers each Feodum is worth 4-5 VP, so the deck greens from $4 hands all game while Provinces come from the $8 hands. Big Money, which cannot use the Silvers, ends 20-40 VP behind.
  • Engines do not get there. Wandering Minstrel discards the Treasures it reveals, Sheepdog is the only draw, Hermit/Madman is a one-shot, and there is no +Buy to spend a big hand on. Every engine seed lost to the money decks by 70-100%.
  • Trashing is a liability. Shaman's mandatory trash gain means every Shaman or Hermit trash hands a card to the opponent; adding Shamans to the leader dropped its mirror win rate to 9-29%.

Method

  1. Audit of the twelve implementations (fixes above).
  2. Archetype seeds — six hand-written theories of the kingdom in dominion/strategy/strategies/bilbao_seeds.py: Fool's Gold rush, Anvil/Feodum slog, Raider money, Hermit/Madman engine, Shaman peddler, Sheepdog/Anvil money. The board-derived island roster had no library reuse and only two weak Wandering Minstrel engine seeds.
  3. Round robin of the seeds plus Big Money (60 games per pairing).
  4. Hand local search on the leader with a parametrised chassis (BilbaoAnvilFeodumVariant, driven by scripts/bilbao_variants.py): five sweeps of 100-400 games per pairing over Anvil count, Feodum timing, Duchy timing, Gold, Fool's Gold cap, Estates, Anvil policy, and every kingdom add-on (Raider, Wheelwright, Sheepdog, Hermit, Shaman, Grotto).
  5. Island-model genetic algorithm — one island per money seed (Anvil/Feodum, Fool's Gold rush, Sheepdog/Anvil), population 20, 12 generations, 16 games per evaluation, fixed panel of the three seeds plus Big Money, run in-process (the shared machine kept killing worker pools).
  6. Confirmation tournament at 400 games per pairing.

Search notes

  • The turn gate on Anvil never fires: three Anvils are always bought before turn 6 (variants with the gate at 6, 14 and 99 played identical games).
  • Feodum ahead of Province never triggers in practice: the Feodum pile is empty before the deck holds 12 Silvers with $8 in hand.
  • Reproduce with PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_bilbao.py --games 400 --strategies "Bilbao Best Found" "Bilbao Anvil Feodum Slog" "Bilbao Fools Gold Rush" BigMoney and the sweeps in scripts/bilbao_variants.py.
  • Evolution agreed with the hand search. The Anvil/Feodum island deleted its Gold, Estate and late-Feodum rules and kept Anvil ×3, Feodum from six Silvers, Fool's Gold before Silver and Duchy from five Provinces; it beat its own seed 61-39 but lost 39-61 to the hand-tuned Feodum-from- three-Silvers / Duchy-from-six version. The Fool's Gold and Sheepdog islands barely moved from their seeds (55-56% vs the panel).
  • The genetic runs had to be run in-process. Parallel islands and worker pools were killed twice by other jobs on the shared machine (BrokenProcessPool with no error of their own); single-process islands with --workers 1 finished.
