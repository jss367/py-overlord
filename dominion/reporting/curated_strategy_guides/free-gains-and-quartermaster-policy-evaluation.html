Free Gains and Quartermaster: Tactical Policy Evaluation

Keep tuned gain preferences by default. Use separate free-gain priorities or contextual overrides when the destination, sacrifice, or current hand changes what you want. The generic fallback is useful coverage, but this experiment found clear Workshop regressions when it replaced purchase preferences.

Measured, with limits. This study compares previous and current policies in 13,200 games across 33 comparisons. Remodel’s joint choices improved the inherited-priority panel. Anvil and Quartermaster changes were mixed. No games reached the 150-round limit. These are exploratory panels, not proof of optimal play.

Practical findings

  • Workshop: inheriting purchase preferences reproduced the control’s results exactly on this panel. Opting into independent generic ranking lowered win scores by 22.5, 10.0, and 6.0 percentage points against the money, Smithy, and engine opponents. All three paired intervals were below zero. Use explicit free-gain priorities rather than assuming that gaining more draw cards will improve a money strategy.
  • Remodel: comparing the whole trash/gain pair with inherited preferences raised win scores by 16.0 points against Big Money (95% paired interval +6.9 to +25.1) and 6.5 against the engine (+0.8 to +12.2). The +7.0 change against Smithy money had an interval spanning zero. Generic independent ranking did not reproduce these gains.
  • Anvil: accounting for the discarded Treasure’s lost income produced changes from −4.0 to +3.0 points with inherited preferences. All intervals spanned zero; regressions are recorded below. Strategies can explicitly override the complete exchange or retain the existing gain and Treasure-discard hooks.
  • Quartermaster: collecting one useful stored card promptly gave mixed changes with inherited priorities. Independent ranking improved the engine matchup by 4.0 points (+1.0 to +7.0), with overlapping intervals elsewhere. Stored Victory points already count toward scoring; the late-game baseline can gain points without first collecting them.
  • Existing Port Moresby strategies: one- and two-Quartermaster money plans were reevaluated against three opponents. Changes ranged from −4.5 to +7.5 points, with every paired interval spanning zero. The Copper Mat Money strategy’s explicit combined override reproduced its control outcomes exactly; its dedicated policy remains useful on the Fountain board.

What was compared

Each comparison uses 100 fixed seeds. For each seed, both policies play both seats against the same opponent, yielding 200 games per policy. A win scores 1, a tie ½, and a loss 0; equal Victory points are broken by fewer turns taken. The two seat outcomes are averaged before uncertainty is calculated, so correlated seats are not treated as independent samples.

Rate intervals use an approximate Wilson bound with a conservative Bernoulli variance bound on the 100 independent seed-pair means. Change intervals use the paired differences and a normal approximation. Comparisons are unadjusted and exploratory; near-boundary rates and the selected small panel limit generalization. Identical observed outcomes produce a zero-width change interval, which should not be read as certainty about unseen seeds.

The common engine uses corrected cost limits, gain reactions, Quartermaster replay queues, and start-of-turn scheduling. The control adapter freezes previous tactical choices. Opponents use current policies. This separates tactical measurement from rules fixes and does not estimate the total effect of upgrading an older engine with incorrect rules.

For each covered card, the representative kingdom contains that card plus Village, Smithy, Laboratory, Market, Festival, Militia, Moat, Watchtower, and Throne Room. Candidate purchases prioritize Provinces, Gold, up to two copies of the covered card, one Smithy, late Duchies, Silver, and late Estates. The inherited variant retains these preferences for free gains. The independent variant uses the shared heuristic without purchase priorities; it is an opt-in stress test, not a searched recommendation. The exact factories and controls are in the reproduction script.

Rules audit and targeted scenarios

The policy tests cover hand support, excessive terminal copies, ownership limits including stored cards, mandatory bad gains, optional declines, empty or invalid menus, early trashing, endgame upgrades, and Watchtower/Trail reactions. Distinct rules defects are tracked separately:

  • Workshop cost limits: reject Potion/debt costs while honoring modified coin costs.
  • Remodel replacement costs: check the trashed card’s current cost after trash reactions, preserve Potion/debt components, and refresh legal gains.
  • Quartermaster recurring instructions: one choice per recurring play, with replays sharing one pile and newly scheduled choices waiting for the next turn.

The official Alchemy rules and Empires rules define componentwise cost comparisons. The Plunder rules specify separate Quartermaster piles and repeated choices on a shared pile when replayed. These tests are targeted interaction coverage, not certification of every card and landscape combination.
