Simulator fixes made before searching

The pre-search audit compared all ten implementations with the printed card text (verified against the Dominion Strategy wiki card data). Rules defects were found and fixed first, with tests in tests/test_kolkata_board_cards.py. Two of them changed the board's whole character:

  1. Every Knight gave the attacker a phantom +$2. The simulator folded "+$2" into the shared Knight attack, so all ten Knights played as Militia-strength terminals. Only Dame Sylvia prints +$2. The attack itself is unchanged: each other player reveals two cards, trashes one costing $3–$6, and discards the rest.
  2. Artificer gained its card into hand. The real card gains "onto your deck". The simulator's version was a free extra card every play; the real one is a topdeck gainer that only pays off when something draws the card.
  3. Sir Martin cost $5 with +1 Buy; the real card costs $4 and gives +2 Buys (so Armory can gain it from the top of the pile).
  4. The attacker chose which revealed card a Knight trashed. "Trashes one of them" is the attacked player's choice. The default victim policy now gives up a revealed Knight first (that also trashes the attacking Knight), then the cheapest qualifying card. Rogue shared the bug and uses the same hook.
  5. Armory always gained the same card. The gain was a hardcoded sort (on this board: Spice Merchant, every time). It now runs the strategy's gain priorities over every exposed $4 pile, the top Knight included.
  6. Scheme could topdeck a Duration. Research stays in play until the next turn, so it is not "discarded from play" and Scheme cannot choose it; the simulator put it on the deck while the Duration was still pending.
  7. Spice Merchant was not optional. With no Copper in hand it trashed a Silver or a Gold. It now only trashes Copper unless a strategy says otherwise.
  8. Strategies could not name the Knights pile. The buyable card is "Sir Bailey" or "Dame Anna", never "Knights", so a rule for "Knights" never fired and the genetic algorithm could not buy or play a Knight. A "Knights" rule now matches whichever Knight is on top of the pile, and count_in_deck("Knights") counts every Knight owned.

Seven decisions were exposed as AI hooks that strategies can override: choose_card_to_trash_for_knight_attack, choose_treasure_to_discard_for_stables (default: Copper, then Spoils, then Silver), choose_treasure_to_trash_for_spice_merchant, choose_spice_merchant_mode, choose_armory_gain, choose_artificer_gain (default: only discard Curses, Victory cards and Coppers, never take a $0 card), and choose_card_to_topdeck_for_scheme.

Board texture

  • Bandit Camp is the only village: +1 Card +2 Actions and a Spoils, a $3 one-shot Treasure that returns to its pile when played.
  • Stables is the draw: discard a Treasure for +3 Cards +1 Action. Coppers and Spoils are Stables fuel, so a Stables deck wants to keep Treasure, not trash it.
  • Artist (8 Debt) is +1 Action and +1 Card per card you have exactly one copy of in play. It rewards a deck of singletons.
  • Knights are the only attack. Each play trashes one of the opponent's top two cards costing $3–$6: Silver, Gold, Duchy and every kingdom card except Artist qualify (Artist costs 8 Debt, so its coin cost is $0). Only Dame Sylvia gives coins; Sir Martin ($4) gives +2 Buys.
  • Trashing comes from Research (trash a card, draw its cost next turn), Spice Merchant (trash a Treasure for +2 Cards +1 Action or +$2 +1 Buy) and Dame Anna.
  • Gainers: Armory (a $4 onto the deck), Artificer (discard N cards, gain a $N card onto the deck), Dame Natalie ($3).
  • Scheme topdecks one Action at Clean-up, so a Knight can attack every turn.
  • Bard is +$2 and a Boon. Buys come only from Spice Merchant and Sir Martin.

Recommendation

Best strategy foundStables and Bard money with one Bandit Camp

Published as Kolkata Best Found, defined in generated_strategies/kolkata_best_found.py.

This is not a formal proof of optimal play. It is the best strategy found by the current simulator, hand-written seeds, island-model genetic evolution, and focused local search around the leader.

  • Buy order: Province at $8; Duchy from four Provinces left; Gold; one Bandit Camp; up to two Stables; one Bard; Estate from two Provinces left; otherwise Silver.
  • Play order: Bandit Camp, then Stables (discard a Copper, else a Spoils, else a Silver, for three cards), then Bard.
  • Spoils: play them like any other Treasure. A policy that saved a Spoils unless it lifted the hand across $5, $6 or $8 was measured neutral (see Validation), so the published strategy keeps the shared default.
  • Never trash Copper. Coppers are Stables fuel. Ignore Research and Spice Merchant.
  • Ignore the Knights. Without the phantom +$2 they are $5 terminals that trash one card per play; every Knights seed and variant lost to the same deck without them.

Why this strategy wins

  • The board is fast. Money reaches eight Provinces in ten or eleven turns per player, so a deck that spends its first five turns on villages and draw is buying Duchies against a wall of Provinces. Every engine seed lost to money, and every engine island evolved into money with Bandit Camps.
  • Stables is a Laboratory that eats a Copper. It never costs an Action and turns the weakest card in hand into three new ones. Two copies are enough: a third stayed within noise.
  • Bandit Camp is a one-card economy. Each play is +1 Card +2 Actions and a $3 Spoils. One copy adds four to eight points over the same deck without it; the genetic run pushed the count to six or seven, but on the Stables chassis one, four and six copies finished within a point of each other at 300 games.
  • Spoils are not worth saving. The intuition that a Spoils kept for a later $5 hand beats one spent on a Silver looked right at 200 and 300 games (57% and 53%) and vanished at 1,000 (50.7%). Two Stables cycle the deck fast enough that a Spoils comes back soon either way.
  • Bard is the cheapest $2. A single terminal is all the deck wants, and the Boon is a small bonus on top. Dropping it cost ten to fifteen points; a second copy was noise.

Method

  1. Audit of the ten implementations (fixes above).
  2. Archetype seeds — thirteen hand-written theories of the kingdom in dominion/strategy/strategies/kolkata_seeds.py: Knights money, Scheme/Knights money, Stables money, Bard money, Spice Merchant money, Armory/Bard money, Bard/Stables money, Stables/Knights money, Bandit Camp money, and four engines (Bandit Camp/Stables, Artificer, Artist singleton, Research).
  3. Seed round robin (80 games per matchup) to rank the theories before evolving them.
  4. Hand local search around the leader (four rounds of 60–200-game round robins): Stables, Bard, Bandit Camp and Knight counts, greening timing, Artificer, Scheme, Spice Merchant, and a Spoils-saving Treasure policy; then a second branch around the Bandit Camp/Scheme slog the Artist island evolved into.
  5. Island-model genetic algorithm — one island per seed (eight hand seeds), plus a board-derived roster and a library-free roster (the two engine archetypes the capability tables enumerate, Big Money, one random island): 20 generations, population 24, 12 games per evaluation, fixed panel of Big Money, Bard Money and Stables Money.
  6. Champion tournament (100 games per matchup), a 200-game round among the leaders of both hand-search branches and the champions, then a 300-game confirmation round including Spoils-policy grafts onto the strongest champions.
  7. Head-to-head confirmation of the published strategy against its Spoils-policy variant, the six-Bandit-Camp variant, the strongest champion graft and Big Money (1,000 games per matchup).

The first version of the Bandit Camp/Stables engine scored 23%: it trashed its own Coppers with Spice Merchant and Research and never bought Gold, so Stables had nothing to discard and the deck had no money. Keeping every Treasure and buying Gold at $6 lifted it to 42%. Games here are short (each player gets ten or eleven turns before the Provinces run out), which is why every engine seed lost to money.

Hand local search

  • Around Bard Stables Money, every single-card change (Stables 1–3, Bard 1–3, Knight, Scheme, Artificer, Spice Merchant, greening timing, refusing to discard Gold to Stables) stayed within 40–58% of the base in 60-game mirrors: noise. The one repeatable signal was adding a Bandit Camp (+4 to +8 points over three rounds), and dropping Bard cost 10–15 points.
  • A Spoils-saving Treasure policy (only play a Spoils when it lifts the hand across $5, $6 or $8) beat the same deck without it 57% of the time at 200 games and 53% at 300, and led every round it entered — then split 50.7% / 49.3% at 1,000 games. It is reported here as a lesson in sample size, not as a finding.
  • The Artist island evolved into a different plan: six Bandit Camps by turn 11, five Schemes that put a Bandit Camp back on the deck every turn, one Bard, and Duchies from six Provinces left. Hand search around it found the same small Spoils-policy signal (+3), and that Scheme count is the fragile lever: with no Bard the deck fills its $3–$4 buys with Schemes, reaches fewer Bandit Camps, and collapses to 12%.

Island run

Every island converged on Bandit Camps, one Bard, Gold and Silver. The hand-seed champions (Bard Stables Money 57.9%, Artist Engine 57.9%, Stables Knights Money 53.7%) all lost to the hand-search leaders (64.1% and 63.3%) in the 100-game champion tournament. The library-free run's engine archetypes evolved into seven-Bandit-Camp money (70% against the panel), which is the same deck the hand search was converging on from the other direction.
