Archived research from the $1 Mining Road model

Dominion research · November 2026

Decision quality and shared lookahead

The earlier Tea House-versus-Shrine comparison measured limited heuristic players. This follow-up saves real decision points, tests alternative legal moves, and adds the same information-limited rollout planner to both strategy families.

Focused planning now improves the tested players in small fresh pilots. It reserves cleanup searches and independently confirms useful Shrine and Spice Merchant choices. The Tea House and Shrine families remain unresolved against each other with planning enabled; these results do not establish optimal play.

The board and starting deck

Two players, eight Provinces, no Colonies. Each starts with Lucky Coin, Survivors, Ruined Village, Ruined Market, Ruined Library, Abandoned Mine, and four Coppers. The Kingdom is Daimyo, Clashes starting with Battle Plan, Fool, Secluded Shrine, Change, Spice Merchant, Highwayman, Lost City, Mining Road, and Tea House. Kind Emperor is the Prophecy; Banquet and Scrounge are Events.

The frozen continuation families are the previous Tea House winner and the corrected Shrine/Spice Merchant build. Their complete settings are in the policy inputs. The original board guide preserves the earlier searches and the 10,000-game comparison.

What the planner can change

  • Choose among legal Actions and Treasures, including stopping a phase.
  • Choose purchases or pass, without requiring the old purchase sequence to remain unchanged.
  • Choose trash targets from the actual legal menu, including good cards that the old junk list never selected.
  • Choose free gains and Kind Emperor gains according to the current position.
  • Choose Spice Merchant's Treasure sacrifice and draw-versus-money mode, and order simultaneous gain effects.

For each candidate, the planner replays the current phase to the exact decision, applies that choice, and plays a sampled continuation to game end. The objective is winning the continuation, rather than minimizing Copper counts or preserving the old purchase. The original live budget was one strategic search and one tactical search per player turn. The cleanup-focused follow-up below replaces those shared slots with separate quotas. Both families receive the same configuration, though actual simulation counts depend on their legal menus.

Information boundaries and exact replay

At a decision, unseen own deck order is resampled. Opponent hand and deck are pooled and resampled while preserving their sizes. Remaining Boon order is resampled. Canonical sorting before sampling prevents the actual hidden arrangement from influencing which sampled worlds are used. Own observed top cards are retained, and top-card memory expires on a shuffle.

A phase checkpoint and decision tape preserve already observed actions and draws. Sampling happens only at the target decision, so earlier observations stay fixed. Replay also checks that the public observation matches the original. This supports choices partway through Shrine or Spice Merchant without losing the remaining card instructions or Mining Road effects. AI objects are replaced on simulated copies; live game state and random state are preserved.

The belief model is deliberately limited: it does not infer hidden cards from an opponent's strategy or maintain every possible opponent reveal. The exercised card-memory paths include Survivors and the board's topdeck hooks. Pile rotation, Boon ordering, and many other ancillary choices still use the continuation policy.

Saved-position audit

From 24 baseline games, the audit retained 72 actual decisions: eight each for Actions, Treasures, purchases, trashing, free gains, Kind Emperor, gain-effect order, Spice Merchant sacrifice, and Spice Merchant mode. Reservoir sampling spreads the audit over the encountered positions. This balances decision categories; it is not a frequency-weighted estimate of whole-game policy weakness.

Each legal alternative initially received 16 sampled continuations. The selected alternative was tested against the baseline on 32 independent sampled worlds. The audit selected a different move in 24 of 72 positions; most apparent gains were uncertain. Two nominally positive positions received an additional independent 256-sample paired confirmation, with Bonferroni-adjusted intervals across those two tests.

Independent verification before switching

The revised live configuration uses four initial samples per candidate and four refinement samples. A proposed departure from the baseline then receives 16 independent paired continuations. It is adopted only when its mean improvement is positive and a one-sided paired sign test reaches 0.05. This is a conservative per-decision acceptance rule, not a guarantee of better play or a whole-game familywise significance claim.

Rule validation exposed by broader search

The engine now enforces Change's mandatory trash and more-expensive gain by default, including fallback handling if an AI declines. Only an explicit --legacy-change-pass research option restores the old menu for reproduction. Unit tests cover both the strict default and the opt-in compatibility behavior.

The strict default completed a four-game, two-planner smoke run with 6,872 sampled continuations and no replay or game-ending errors. That checks the corrected path; four games are insufficient for a strength comparison. The legacy pilots above do not justify replacing the previous winner.

Follow-up: reserve searches for cleanup and purchases

The original shared slots were consumed too early: an Action could use the tactical slot before Shrine's trash choices, and a Kind Emperor gain could use the strategic slot before purchasing. The new cleanup-focused schedule reserves separate per-player-turn quotas. It searches one purchase, one gain, up to two trash choices (enough for one Shrine trigger), one Treasure play-or-stop choice while Shrine is armed, and one Spice Merchant mode choice. Actions, gain-effect order, and Spice Merchant's Treasure sacrifice retain the continuation heuristic in this profile. The older schedule remains available for reproduction.

Discovery starts with four sampled worlds for every legal alternative. In batches of four, it gives the baseline and current leaders up to twelve extra worlds, stopping early for a promising alternative or a baseline that wins every sampled continuation. Candidate comparisons use matching world indices even when the leaders change. A proposed switch then receives 32 independent paired continuations; adoption still requires a positive mean and a one-sided paired sign-test result at 0.05. Adaptive stopping only proposes a choice; it never bypasses that independent gate.

The planner settings were frozen before screening. Both strategy families receive the same settings. This package also increases the available searches and verification budget, so a performance difference cannot be attributed to scheduling alone. The named schedule replaces the old numeric decision budget; fixed refinement is unused when adaptive refinement is enabled.

Independent decision checks under strict rules

A new stratified sample retained 84 positions from 32 baseline games: twelve each for Treasures, purchases, trashing, free gains, Kind Emperor, Spice Merchant's sacrifice, and Spice Merchant's mode. Discovery proposed alternatives in 29 positions. Fresh 64-world checks found two nominally positive cases and eight negative ones; the remaining estimates were uncertain or unchanged. These raw proposals are not adopted live moves. This is direct evidence that independent verification is necessary at the cheap discovery budget.

Both positive cases received another 256 independent paired worlds, with Bonferroni-adjusted 95% intervals across the two confirmations. All these new experiments use mandatory Change rules.

An adopted Shrine choice survives fresh confirmation

The 16-game Shrine screen searched 108 trash decisions and adopted one change: decline a Copper trash in seed 22,010,005, swapped seats, turn 10. At that decision the player had $9 and two Buys; the remaining hand contained two Coppers, one Silver, and one Province. The independent live gate had favored keeping the card. To check selection noise, the confirmation runner reproduced the exact game and decision, then tested every adopted trash change from that screen on 256 new paired worlds using a sample stream separate from discovery, live verification, and the earlier audit.

Trashing the Copper won 67.97% of those continuations; passing won 96.09%. The estimated gain was 28.13 percentage points, 95% interval 22.50–33.75. There was one adopted trash change in the screen, so the multiple-comparison adjustment covered one test. This establishes a useful conditional Shrine improvement under the continuation model. It supports choosing when to trash, rather than assuming either that every Copper should go or that cleanup is always unnecessary.

In the fresh Shrine mirror, the planner searched 213 trash choices and 79 Spice Merchant mode choices. It adopted two trash changes, two mode changes, and three Treasure choices, as well as purchase and gain changes. Across all 96 fresh games it ran 277,012 sampled continuations. The new schedule therefore reaches cleanup decisions in live play, rather than only in an offline audit. Whole-player gains cannot be assigned specifically to trashing: purchases and Kind Emperor choices also changed, and the search budget increased.

Strategy recommendation: retain Tea House/Banquet as the practical starting family, with contextual gains and cleanup choices. The old claim should be “best tested heuristic family,” not “optimal play never trashes.” The new evidence supports decision-specific planning and leaves the best cleanup investment unresolved. A larger frozen-policy comparison would improve precision; a richer continuation policy is still needed to test coordinated departures from the heuristics.

What remains restricted

  • After the candidate move, rollouts use fixed continuation policies rather than recursively planning every later decision. Combinations needing several coordinated deviations may be missed.
  • The opponent model is the copied continuation policy. It does not forecast a future opponent that itself replans.
  • The focused schedule protects cleanup and purchase searches but leaves Actions, effect order, and later choices beyond each quota at their heuristic defaults. The first gain can still consume the shared gain quota, and other trash effects can consume the two trash slots before Shrine.
  • Small samples can misrank alternatives. Independent verification filters proposals but also rejects some genuine small improvements.
  • The position sampler is stratified by decision type and covers only this scenario and the frozen continuation families.
