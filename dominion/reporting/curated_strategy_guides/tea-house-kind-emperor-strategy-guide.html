Board strategy guide

Tea House and Kind Emperor

The strongest strategy family found is Fortune Hunter/Silver into Tea House, free City Quarters, Imperial Envoy, and early Provinces. Two nearly identical versions finished effectively tied. This is a simulation recommendation, not a proof of optimal play.

A subsequent dedicated Mine and Guildhall search tested 634 candidate configurations. Its strongest confirmed candidate won 43.1% against the published engine over 2,000 games. That follow-up supports keeping this recommendation and gives much stronger evidence about Mine/Guildhall than the initial screen below.

Assumptions: two players, seven Coppers and three Estates to start, ordinary Province supply, no Platinum or Colony. Kind Emperor is the Prophecy; Guildhall is a Project, not a Kingdom pile. The ten Kingdom cards are recorded in the board file.

How to play it

  1. Open Fortune Hunter/Silver on $4/$3. Buy Tea House on a $5/$2 opening and skip the $2 purchase. Courier/Silver is playable but was weaker in the confirmation games.
  2. Prioritize your first two Tea Houses. They cycle and provide money while advancing Kind Emperor. There is no general-purpose thinning here: Courier does not trash cards, and Mine only exchanges Treasures.
  3. Use Kind Emperor to supply City Quarters without buying them. When you lack a City Quarter in hand, gain one directly to hand. When you already have one, take an Imperial Envoy until you own two, then prefer Tea House. If a desired pile is empty, choose the next useful Action. The free gain is mandatory.
  4. Buy Provinces early. Do not wait to assemble a large engine. The published version takes an affordable Province once it owns one Tea House; waiting for two was statistically indistinguishable. Between Province purchases, fill out Tea House income and up to two Imperial Envoys. The policy buys at most four Tea Houses, although free gains can increase that number.
  5. Play City Quarter before other Actions to maximize its draw and supply Actions for Imperial Envoy. Tea Houses then provide income; Imperial Envoy provides draw and another Buy at the expense of two debt. Fortune Hunter adds money. Plan purchases using money left after paying debt.
  6. Track the Province race and empty piles. The tested policy starts favoring Duchies when three Provinces remain. In human play, count points before ending the game; forced Action gains can empty a third pile.

The key rules interaction is that Kind Emperor can gain any Action, regardless of cost. Gaining City Quarter does not incur its eight-debt purchase cost. This makes it possible to add draw and Actions while spending money on income and points. See the publisher's Rising Sun rules and Empires rules.

What the simulations established

The search played 37,760 games: 7,080 in an initial screen, 12,480 in a combination search, 11,200 in an eight-finalist tournament, and 7,000 in independent confirmation. Initial screening was exploratory; a Fortune Hunter shuffle correction preceded the combination search and both confirmation stages.

Each finalist matchup used 400 games. The one-Tea-House Province threshold scored 55.25% across its seven opponents; the two-Tea-House threshold scored 55.18%. Those results do not separate the two policies. Both beat the slower variants on average.

Fresh-seed confirmation of the published one-Tea-House version used 1,000 games per opponent:

Opponent Published strategy's win rate
Same engine, waiting for two Tea Houses before Provinces 48.6%
Courier opening with two Imperial Envoys 54.7%
Fortune Hunter engine adding two Masterminds 53.6%
Fortune Hunter engine prioritizing Duchies and Dukes 84.0%
Tea House engine investing in Guildhall and four Buried Treasures 65.3%
Imperial Envoy treasure strategy 74.2%
Basic treasure strategy 93.6%

Seats alternate; each shuffle seed is used in both seats. Equal scores use the official fewer-turns tiebreak; complete ties count as half a win. No confirmation games reached the turn limit. Approximate sampling uncertainty for a 1,000-game result near 50% is three percentage points, before allowing for dependence between paired games. The small differences among the best engine variants should therefore be treated cautiously.

Practical takeaway: prioritize the engine's basic components and timely Provinces. Mastermind and Buried Treasure are optional additions, not prerequisites. Guildhall and a committed Duchy–Duke plan lost clearly in these tested implementations. Always taking City Quarter with the Prophecy was also substantially weaker than choosing according to the current hand.

Reproduction and simulator corrections

The registered strategy implementation is named Tea House and Kind Emperor Engine. Its decision hooks implement the policy; its static gain list records referenced cards and is not a literal unconditional purchase order. The class parameters preserve the alternatives used in the search.

Run from the repository root:

PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_tea_house_kind_emperor.py tournament
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_tea_house_kind_emperor.py validation

The script uses six game worker processes. Raw results, candidate parameters, and shuffle seed blocks are saved in the finalist tournament and independent validation. The screening scripts and results remain in the workspace's .context directory.

Corrections needed before relying on this board:

  • Anvil's optional discard and gain resolve when played; it cannot gain a card with debt in its cost. After choosing to discard, the gain is mandatory. See Prosperity rules.
  • Courier discards the top card and may play an Action or Treasure from anywhere in the discard pile. It does not trash the revealed card. See Allies rules.
  • Buried Treasure plays itself when gained; both its extra Buy and $3 arrive next turn. Resolved Durations stay in play until cleanup. Mine must not move an already-played Buried Treasure back into hand. Fortune Hunter must preserve existing top cards when shuffling to look at three cards. See Plunder rules.

Focused regression tests cover these interactions. The full suite passed after the corrections (2,115 passed, one skipped); the final additional Fortune Hunter regression is checked separately. A callable-signature test failed intermittently and also reproduced on the unchanged checkout before passing on the full rerun.

The search compares implemented buying and tactical policies. It does not exhaust every opening, optional card decision, Duration combination, or endgame plan, and it does not establish the best strategy for different player counts or Colony games.
