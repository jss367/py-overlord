Recruiter, Kitsune, and the Courtyard race

Build around Courtyard draw, Kitsune's attack, Recruiter thinning, and Treasurer's money and Key. Once Great Leader activates, Courtyards can be chained without spending Villagers. Inventor discounts and Villa gains help convert that deck into points.

Prophecy: Great Leader. Use two players, normal seven-Copper/three-Estate starting decks, eight Provinces, ten Curses, and the Key artifact. Great Leader starts with five Sun tokens. Each Kitsune removes one; after activation, each Action played gives an additional action. No Colony, Platinum, Potion, Shelters, or other landscapes are included. Best strategy found

The Kitsune-first Courtyard engine led the twelve-policy round robin with 65.6%, counting ties as half a win. Fresh validation scored 53.3% against the Counterfeit runner-up and 50.0% against the six-Courtyard engine. The evidence supports the engine family; it does not establish that an eight-copy target is superior to six.
  1. Open Silver and Courtyard on $4/$3; Kitsune and Courtyard on $5/$2. The same purchases apply in either hand order. On $4/$3, the first hand buys Silver and the other buys Courtyard.
  2. Take Kitsune at the first $5, then one Recruiter and one Treasurer. Kitsune starts the Curse race and advances Great Leader. Recruiter removes junk and supplies Villagers; Treasurer provides money and contests the Key.
  3. Contest the Courtyard pile. The winning policy targets eight, while the six-copy variant is equally competitive head-to-head. These are acquisition targets, not guaranteed deck contents: opponents usually contest the same ten-card pile.
  4. Add Inventor and useful Villa gains after the draw package. Secondary targets are three Inventors, a second Kitsune, and two Villas, with at most two Silvers and two Golds as lower priorities. Villa may exceed its target when gaining it immediately rescues playable actions.
  5. Turn toward points after ten completed turns, or when four Provinces remain. Province then takes first priority. Duchy becomes a priority at four Provinces; Estate at two. Spare gains and buys can take additional points once desired deck pieces are unavailable. Take an immediate winning pile ending when available.

The recommendation does not normally buy Village, Counterfeit, Anvil, or Engineer. They remain on the requested board and were tested in alternative policies. This is evidence about these policies and opponents, not proof that those cards can never help.

How to play the key cards

Before Great Leader: play available villages first, use Kitsune's actions when needed to continue, and spend Recruiter's Villagers to play draw. After activation: chain Courtyards freely. Keep playable draw and useful money in hand; usually put a Curse, Victory card, or Copper back on top with Courtyard. With no way to continue playing actions, put back a useful stranded action for the next turn.

Recruiter: trash Curse, Estate, then Copper before useful cards. Avoid playing it without expendable cards once thinning is complete. Play it before Inventor when possible: cost reduction also reduces the Villagers received from trashing. Kitsune: usually take Curse plus money while Curses remain, substituting actions when necessary before Great Leader. After Curses run out, use money and actions.

Treasurer: take the Key when you do not hold it. Otherwise recover a useful Treasure from the trash, or trash Copper. Inventor: gains become stronger as discounts accumulate; the discount applies only after that play's gain. Villa: a gain supplies an action and puts Villa in hand; gaining it in the Buy phase resumes the Action phase, so it can rescue unplayed Courtyards. Watch the Curse, Courtyard, Villa, and Victory piles for three-pile endings.

Rules corrected before every reported experiment

The card implementations were checked before searching, because several discrepancies materially changed this board:

  • Kitsune now grants two actions in its action mode and resolves selected modes in printed order. Rising Sun rules.
  • Engineer costs four Debt and gains only one additional card when trashed. Its gain choices use current prices and exclude Debt/Potion costs. Villa grants one action on gain, moves a topdecked gain to hand, and returns to Actions only during its owner's Buy phase, including Treasure play. Anvil gaining Villa now preserves that return. Empires rules.
  • Inventor no longer grants an unprinted action; its gains exclude Debt costs and follow supply-pile boundaries. Recruiter awards Villagers from the trashed card's current coin cost, including Inventor reductions. Renaissance rules.

The strategy also has explicit Courtyard topdeck choices, Engineer trash decisions, and Counterfeit targets, forwarded through the normal game AI. Regression tests cover the corrected rules and chained Buy/Action transitions.

Reproduce and inspect

Run from the repository root. Each recorded match includes its full parameter dictionaries, game count, and seed. Games use paired seeds with swapped seats. The search defaults to six worker processes; use --workers 1 for serial execution.

PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_recruiter_kitsune.py search --games 32 --output .context/recruiter-search.json
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_recruiter_kitsune.py refine --input .context/recruiter-search.json --games 64 --output .context/recruiter-refinement.json
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_recruiter_kitsune.py tournament --input .context/recruiter-refinement.json --finalists 8 --games 200 --output .context/recruiter-tournament.json
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/search_recruiter_kitsune.py validate --input .context/recruiter-tournament.json --games 600 --output .context/recruiter-validation.json
PYTHONPATH=. python -m dominion.simulation.strategy_battle "Recruiter Kitsune Courtyard Engine" "Recruiter Kitsune Counterfeit Money" --board boards/recruiter_kitsune.txt --games 600 --workers 6
