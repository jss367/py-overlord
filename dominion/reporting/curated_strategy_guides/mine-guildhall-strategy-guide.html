Dedicated combo investigation

Mine and Guildhall

The combo is real, but the dedicated search did not find a version that beats the existing Tea House / City Quarter / Imperial Envoy engine. The strongest confirmed candidate won 43.1% against that engine. A version that acquired both Mine and Guildhall in over 90% of games won 38.3%.

These results use the same two-player board, Estate starts, and Province supply as the original strategy guide. They supersede the initial, lightly tested Mine/Guildhall recommendation.

What was explored

The follow-up evaluated 634 candidate configurations, including equivalent settings, across 65,792 scored games:

Stage Scope Games
Initial screen 448 configurations, 32 games each 14,336
Recheck 24 screened finalists, 400 games each 9,600
Focused refinement 186 configurations, 96 games each 17,856
Confirmation and controlled comparisons Ten matchups, 2,000 games each 20,000
Upgrade-order confirmation Two additional finalists, 2,000 games each 4,000

Choices included Mine-first and Guildhall-first setups; one to three Mines; zero to two additional Masterminds; Kind Emperor gaining Mine early; Copper-first, Silver-first, and Gold-recycling policies; Mine gains of Buried Treasure; Mine before or after Imperial Envoy; different Province timing; and lighter or heavier Tea House investment. Fixed setup sequences can require a Mastermind even when the subsequent acquisition target is zero.

The audit records actual ownership of both pieces, acquisition timing, Mine's individual upgrades, Mastermind targets, and Coffers generated. This matters: a strategy that plans to buy Guildhall can end the game before buying it.

The additional Copper-first version of the early-Mine policy won 33.5%. None of these confirmation intervals reaches 50%.

The 43.1% candidate is saved as Mine And Guildhall Engine in the strategy module. Its practical plan is:

  1. Open Fortune Hunter/Silver on $4/$3, or Tea House/skip on $5/$2.
  2. Prioritize two Tea Houses, one Mine, and Guildhall in that order. Begin prioritizing affordable Provinces from turn six, so some games never reach the Guildhall purchase.
  3. Use Kind Emperor for the first Mine when missing; thereafter gain City Quarter when absent from hand, then build to two Imperial Envoys and add Tea Houses.
  4. With Mine, prefer Silver→Gold, then Copper→Silver. Recycle Gold into Gold only with Guildhall.
  5. Draw with Imperial Envoy before playing Mine. Buy up to four Tea Houses and supplement with Silver as needed; Mine-created Silvers count toward the target.

This is the strongest confirmed candidate from this investigation, not a claim that all possible Mine/Guildhall policies have been exhausted.

What removing the pieces showed

A controlled comparison held the Tea House→Mine→Guildhall framework and its early-Mine play order fixed, then removed individual investments. This is the 33.7% candidate above, not the 43.1% candidate.

Version Win rate against the existing champion
Mine and Guildhall 33.7%
Mine, skipping Guildhall 34.3%
Guildhall, skipping Mine 34.8%
Skip both 50.8%

There is nevertheless evidence of synergy in a direct matchup: the combo beat the corresponding Mine-without-Guildhall version 52.7% of the time (95% interval 50.7–54.7%). Against its own version with both pieces removed, the combo won only 35.9%. Each direct matchup also used 2,000 games and separate seeds.

These results support a modest benefit from Guildhall in that particular Mine matchup. They do not show that Guildhall improves every Mine deck, or that investing in both pieces is competitive with the faster engine.

Why it falls behind here

In the 38.3% dedicated build, Mine arrived around turn 5.2 and Guildhall around turn 6.6, among games acquiring each. Games ended around round 13.0. Across all games, Mine produced about 2.94 treasure gains and Guildhall generated 2.64 Coffers. The Project's $5 investment usually had little time to pay back, and both purchases competed with income and draw development.

Mastermind can genuinely multiply the interaction: Copper→Silver→Gold→Gold produces three Guildhall triggers. The regression tests verify this sequence. The early Mastermind build made about 4.96 Mine gains per game, but won only 24.4%; its additional setup did not catch up with the champion's Province race.

The interpretation is a timing problem on this board, not an absence of synergy. Mine improves Treasure quality and Guildhall rewards its gains, but Kind Emperor supplies the competing engine's villages for free while Tea House supplies immediate income and draw. The original engine remains the practical recommendation.

The underlying gain rules are documented in the publisher's base-game rules and Renaissance rules.

Reproduction and validation

Run from the repository root:

PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_mine_guildhall.py screen
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_mine_guildhall.py recheck
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_mine_guildhall.py refine
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_mine_guildhall.py confirm
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/search_mine_guildhall.py upgrades

All parameters, seeds, outcomes, ownership counts, upgrade counts, and confidence intervals are preserved in the screen, recheck, refinement, confirmation, and upgrade confirmation files. The scripts use six worker processes. Each seed is played in both starting seats. Complete ties earn half a win; equal scores first use the fewer-turns tiebreak.

The 20,000-game confirmation was repeated after strengthening the end-condition audit, and every recorded outcome matched exactly. This repetition is not counted as additional evidence or included in the 65,792 total. All final confirmation games ended naturally; the maximum length in the main confirmation was 21 rounds.

Additional simulator work separated Mine's trash/gain decisions from ordinary Treasure play, added a dedicated Mastermind target choice, and allowed Coffers to pay debt before purchases. The old simulator's inability to use Coffers for debt could penalize Guildhall with Imperial Envoy. All comparisons in this follow-up use the correction on both sides. The full test suite passed: 2,119 passed, one skipped; focused regressions cover debt payment and the triple-Mine sequence.
