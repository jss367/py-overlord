Strategy Retirement Review

Eight strategies qualified for retirement in this review. The archive contains those eight strategies plus two historical comparison opponents removed from the active pool upstream; the current active count is shown in the catalog. This review played 50,600 scored games. Archived code, aliases, and historical report links remain available.

The saved cross-board leaderboard contained 84 entrants, while the current loader discovered 142. Its lowest ranks provided candidates for investigation; the decisions below use fresh comparisons on fixed boards.

Retirement decisions

These replacements apply to the evaluated boards. The separate Big Money comparison and strategy removal also retired the original and optimized custom-board engines. All four custom-board implementations are archived here so the recorded experiments and links remain reproducible; Big Money is their active replacement.

Version 26 stays active: it won 39% in the 200-game screen but 48.3% in the independent 1,000-game confirmation. Versions 22 and 27 also stay active after screening at 43% and 50.5%. A small screen is insufficient evidence for retirement.

Keep the Big Money benchmark: the historical custom-board comparison engine won only 9.8% against it with Estates and 5.2% with Shelters. Big Money won 90.2% and 94.8% respectively, so it remains a stronger benchmark on this board.

How the decisions were made

  1. Screen eight Torturer-family variants, two weak custom-board entries, and the weakest generated Village/Smithy strategy. Test the two custom-board entries with both Estate and Shelter starts.
  2. Advance candidates below 40% against their proposed replacement to a separate 1,000-game confirmation. Stage-specific, deterministic seeds keep confirmation games separate from screening.
  3. Require the upper end of the candidate's 95% Wilson interval against the replacement to be below 40%.
  4. Compare both candidate and replacement against the same additional opponents. For each opponent, require the candidate's upper Wilson bound minus the replacement's lower Wilson bound to be no more than five percentage points. Retain uncertain or useful specialists.
  5. For the custom-board entries, require the criteria to pass with both Estate and Shelter starts. Preserve baseline opponents and strategies outside the evaluated shortlist.

The comparison panel includes Big Money, a generated Patrol/Patrician Big Money opponent, and the competitive Torturer version 27 for the Torturer family; Big Money and the original custom-board strategy for the Patrician family; and Smithy Big Money plus Village/Smithy/Laboratory for the base-card candidate. The intervals are per comparison, not a simultaneous confidence guarantee over all strategies.

Fixed boards and simulator boundaries

  • Torturer family: the repository's Torture Campaign board, including Ride and Way of the Butterfly.
  • Patrician family: Collection, Emporium, Forager, Miser, Modify, Patrician, Rats, Rebuild, Skulk, and Snowy Village, with Looting and Sewers enabled. This explicit scenario includes the landscapes referenced by the original strategies. Both Estate and Shelter starts were evaluated.
  • Village/Smithy candidate: Chapel, Festival, Laboratory, Market, Mine, Moat, Smithy, Village, Witch, and Workshop, with Estate starts and no landscapes.

The original anchors were Torturer version 25, the now-archived optimized Patrician/Collection engine, and Chapel Witch. All games use the repository simulator and two players. Supply and landscapes stay fixed within a cohort. Every entrant is checked for board compatibility before play. The simulator resolves equal victory points by fewer turns and assigns a remaining full tie by seat order; seats alternate, and the reported rates use that existing awarded-win convention. No gameplay logic changed for this review.

Archive behavior and reproduction

Archived factories remain in their original modules. Default tournament lists, automatic strategy reuse, active catalog cards, and board recommendations exclude them. Explicit lookup by the original name or alias still works. Historical strategy pages display their archived status and replacement. Removing a factory's retirement decorator restores it to default discovery.

Catalog regeneration preserves the latest saved cross-board standings and marks their freshness using the current renderer. This review does not rerun that tournament; upstream updated it separately. Links to archived entries remain available.

Source files, raw evidence, and commands

Machine-readable evidence: screening results and confirmation and opponent-panel results. Every row records the exact board, starting-deck choice, seed, opponents, and game count. Strategy names in these files are the existing loader identifiers.

The runner is scripts/audit_strategy_retirement.py. The strategy implementations and simulator were evaluated from source revision 0c260353d464dd96567483052acb40a46e4703c0; this change adds retirement metadata without altering play policies.

PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/audit_strategy_retirement.py --stage screen \
  --output .context/retirement-reproduction-screen.json --workers 8
PYTHONPATH=. python scripts/audit_strategy_retirement.py --stage validate \
  --screen .context/retirement-reproduction-screen.json \
  --output .context/retirement-reproduction-validation.json --workers 8

Use fresh output paths to replay games; an existing output resumes completed pairings only when its revision, Python version, and source fingerprint match. Historical evidence without a fingerprint is preserved for inspection but cannot be resumed. Both stages must use the same source and runtime; edits during evaluation abort before appending results. Use at least two workers for deterministic per-game seeding. Worker count does not change the per-game seed schedule. These commands remain valid after retirement because explicit lookup is preserved.
