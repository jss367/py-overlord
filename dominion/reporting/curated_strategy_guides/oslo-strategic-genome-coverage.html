October 14, 2026 · Search representation audit

Oslo: Search Policy Coverage and Reference Equivalence

Both strong references can now be expressed by the typed search policy.

The compiled policies matched every recorded decision and game outcome in 200 paired games: 400 simulator runs and 62,070 decision comparisons. This establishes coverage on the audited games and boundary scenarios. It does not establish that a search run will discover these configurations.

Why extend the search space?

The earlier three-seed reflection pilot improved on its genetic control but still lost more often than it won against the strong Oslo references. Its original JSON schema could not express important reference decisions. That limited what either genetic mutation or reflective proposals could achieve.

Schema 2 adds reusable fields to the existing compiler and genetic operators. The reference classes remain unchanged. Their audit encodings compile into fresh base strategies; they do not inherit a reference class or call its custom predicates or decision hooks. The normal reflective experiment continues to start from board-derived seeds. These explicit reference encodings are audit fixtures.

Each reference and its compiled counterpart played against Big Money and the other strong reference. For each opponent, 25 shuffle seeds were tested in both seats. Each paired game ran the original and compiled policy separately with the same seed, seat, board and opponent. The audit compared the full ordered sequence of public decision requests and returned choices for both players, then winner, scores and final turn count. Nested implementation calls were excluded because the generic compiler and custom reference hook can delegate differently while making the same simulator-facing decision.

This is a finite regression audit, rather than a proof over all possible game states. Boundary tests separately cover changing prices, exact affordability and buy limits, commitment and fallback timing, first-gain opening limits, dependency counts, purchase balance, unequal gain ratios, gain caps and ties, empty gain piles, multiplier payload selection, export/reload, fingerprints, mutation and crossover. The equivalence games test behavior preservation; they are not fresh evidence of a strength advantage.
