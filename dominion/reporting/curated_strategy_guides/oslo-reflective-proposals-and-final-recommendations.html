Oslo: Reflective Proposals and Final Search Recommendations

The study did not resolve a consistent benefit from further genetic search.

The original GEPA proposals averaged 67.0% wins against the five-opponent panel; the final assisted recommendations averaged 73.4%, and genetic-only controls averaged 58.2%.

Final recommendations changed the panel win rate by +6.5 percentage points versus their proposals (95% interval across search runs: -1.0 to +14.0 points). 2 of 5 final recommendations were identical to their original proposals.

This follow-up reused the five saved runs from the shared-budget benchmark. GEPA is the reflective optimization method that revised typed strategy plans using simulator feedback. The original proposal is GEPA's selected plan before genetic training. The final assisted recommendation was selected from the assisted search's starting policies and genetic champion. It can be a different starting policy or a mutation; this comparison does not establish ancestry from the proposal.

What this tells us about the next experiment

This diagnostic measures the quality of particular saved proposals and recommendations. It does not compare equally funded GEPA-only and assisted algorithms: the original proposals consumed much less search computation, and the assisted procedure can fall back to another starting seed. The runs were inspected in the previous study, so this is a post-hoc follow-up with fresh shuffle evidence, rather than five new independent search runs.

The next algorithm comparison should give a proposal-only arm its own explicit budget for additional reflective proposals and validation, then compare it with assisted and genetic-only arms on new search seeds. Keep all three recommendations eligible during validation and lock them before a separate test block. Additional kingdoms are needed before choosing a general default; these findings apply to the Oslo board and this policy representation.
