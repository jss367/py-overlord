Shepherd and Tragic Hero

A compact draw-and-money plan, tested against the Night-card engines available in this kingdom.

Kingdom: Faithful Hound, Guardian, Herbalist, Monastery, Secret Cave, Exorcist, Shepherd, Cobbler, Crypt, Tragic Hero.

Assumptions: two players, eight Provinces, no Colony, Platinum, Shelters or landscapes. Because Shepherd and Secret Cave are present, both players start with five Coppers, Magic Lamp, Pasture and three Estates, even if they never buy either Action.

Updated recommendation: a dedicated follow-up found a stronger Cobbler plan that gains extra Shepherds based on the hand. Read the new Cobbler and Shepherd comparison. The original study and its results remain below for reference.
Original search winner: 1 Shepherd, 1 Tragic Hero. Build Gold and Silver behind the draw cards, keep your starting Estates for Shepherd and Pasture, and replace Tragic Hero when it trashes itself.

This was the strongest policy selected by the original bounded search, not a proof of optimal play. The comparisons below show how much separates it from close alternatives.

How to play the original plan

Open Shepherd / Silver on a four-coin / three-coin split. The tested policy also buys Shepherd on a five-coin opening and passes on the two-coin turn. It does not buy a cheap card just to spend the money.

  1. Province whenever affordable. There is no deliberate delay to build a larger engine.
  2. With three or fewer Provinces remaining, take Duchy at five or more coins; otherwise take Estate at two or more. These buys take priority over further deck building.
  3. Before that scoring switch, maintain one Shepherd, then one Tragic Hero. Buy the first affordable unmet target; this also replaces a Hero that has trashed itself.
  4. Once both draw targets are met, buy Gold at six coins, Silver at three to five. Otherwise pass. The tested plan does not buy the other eight kingdom piles.
  5. Use Tragic Hero’s extra buy for another affordable card under the same priorities. Late Estates are especially useful: with Pasture, each adds two victory points.

Close alternatives: starting Estate buys at two Provinces remaining scored almost identically to the recommendation in fresh validation (50.5% for the recommendation; interval 49.3%–51.7%). Adding one Cobbler also remained close (48.0% for the recommendation; interval 44.5%–51.5%). That original test did not separate Cobbler’s free-gain targets from purchase targets; the follow-up linked above supersedes the compact-plan default.

Play Shepherd before Tragic Hero. Discard Victory cards, including Pasture when useful for draw. Pasture also makes each Estate worth two victory points in total while you still own it. Tragic Hero gives an extra buy; if its draw trashes it, the gained Gold goes to your discard pile. Replace the Hero according to the ownership target.

Play Magic Lamp after one of each Treasure name and before duplicates. It requires six cards with exactly one copy each in play. Duplicated names do not count, and retained Duration cards can count. Wishes can supply Gold or needed support cards directly to hand. Do not delay the main plan just to force the Lamp.

What the other cards offer

CardRole and tradeoff
Faithful HoundCheap terminal draw; Secret Cave or Ghost can discard it to improve the next hand. Extra terminals compete with Tragic Hero for the available Action.
GuardianA delayed coin, including when bought and played immediately. There are no Attack cards in this kingdom, so its protection has no target here.
HerbalistExtra buying and Treasure topdecking, but uses the Action that could play draw.
MonasteryCan remove played Coppers without spending an Action. Buying it early competes with economy; the search also tested buying it only after money priorities.
Secret CaveCan trade spare Victory cards and Hounds for next-turn income. It adds timing decisions and is not automatically worth buying just because Magic Lamp is in the starting deck.
ExorcistProvides a route to Spirits: Estates can become Wisps, Silver can become Imp, and a card costing at least five coins can become Ghost. Its tempo cost is substantial.
ShepherdTurns starting Estates and later scoring cards into draw without consuming the available Action.
CobblerSupplies cards to hand on later turns, but the initial five-coin purchase competes with Tragic Hero.
CryptBanks played Treasures and returns them over future turns. This can provide steadier Gold income, but buying too much support can slow scoring.
Tragic HeroMain terminal draw, an extra buy, and eventual Gold. Its self-trashing makes the target an ownership count, not a lifetime purchase limit.

Search scope and simulator audit

Two broad screens used separate random candidate sets and seeds, with 157 candidate entries per run. Each candidate played 32 games against each of four reference plans. Focused tuning varied card counts, opening preferences, purchase timing and scoring thresholds. The final round robin used 400 games per pairing. The champion was selected before fresh validation and support-card comparisons.

  • First broad search: 20,096 games.
  • Second broad search: 20,096 games.
  • Focused tuning: 22,080 games.
  • Finalist round robin: 18,000 games.
  • Independent validation: 8,000 games.
  • Support-card comparisons: 6,600 games.

The initial audit found errors that would bias this kingdom. Corrections cover Exorcist’s Spirit gains; Crypt’s use of played Treasures; Guardian’s gain destination; Magic Lamp’s singleton test and Wish setup; Ghost’s two plays on the next turn; Tragic Hero’s normal gain destination; Wish replay behavior; repeated Secret Cave bonuses; and ownership of set-aside cards. The audit used the publisher’s Nocturne rules, with regression tests for the relevant interactions.

The search uses fixed tactical rules, limited card-count targets and fixed start-of-turn ordering. It does not exhaust all legal play or optimize every multi-buy ending. More sophisticated Ghost, Imp or Secret Cave play may outperform the tested versions. Early debugging runs were discarded and are excluded from the reported total.

Evidence: search summary, complete finalist tournament, validation results, support-card comparisons.
