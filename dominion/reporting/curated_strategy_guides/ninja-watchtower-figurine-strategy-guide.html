Two-player Dominion · September 18, 2026

Ninja, Watchtower
and Figurine

Build a compact Action package and let Treasure-phase draw carry the economy. The selected policy takes Gold ahead of its last Figurine and adds a single Silk Merchant — the two settings that separate it from the version published on September 17.

Recommendation: open Ninja / Watchtower on a $4/$3 split. Buy Platinum and Gold before finishing your Figurines, take one Silk Merchant, then Pendant and Silver. Buy Colonies whenever you can and collect Museum names at the end. Engines built on Stables and Harbor Village lose to this plan even after their play was corrected.

Your board

Catapult/Rocks · Watchtower · Conclave · Harbor Village · Innkeeper · Ninja · Silk Merchant · Figurine · Pendant · Stables.

Landscapes: Credit and Museum. Base piles: Platinum and Colony included. Assumptions: two players, seven Coppers and three Estates each, no Shelters or additional landscapes. Catapult is the standard split pile: five Catapults above five Rocks.

How to play it

1. Small Action package

One Ninja and one Watchtower, both from the opening. Ninja pressures the opponent; Watchtower restores a small hand after an attack. Nothing else terminal goes in except one Silk Merchant.

2. Money before the last Figurine

Three Figurines draw during the Treasure phase without spending Actions, but Platinum and Gold come first when you can afford them. Buying the third Figurine ahead of Gold is the single biggest mistake the previous version made.

3. Score and diversify

Take Colonies at $11, Provinces once four or fewer Colonies remain or from turn 22, and pick up missing card names for Museum at the end.

Opening details: on $4/$3 or $3/$4, buy Ninja on the $4 turn and Watchtower on the $3 turn. The exact tested policy also buys Ninja on a $5/$2 split and passes at $2. Credit openings were explored, but the selected policy does not use Credit. A dedicated search conditioned on each opening split was not performed.

Turn sequencing that matters

  • Watchtower versus Ninja: with only one Action, you often must choose. The tested policy plays Watchtower first when the hand has five or fewer cards, otherwise Ninja. Ninja may be played from the deck, but still consumes an Action. Watchtower does not block Ninja’s discard attack.
  • Anything that refunds an Action goes before a terminal. If you do pivot into Stables or Innkeeper, play those first: Watchtower and Silk Merchant end the Action phase, so leading with one strands whatever else the hand is holding. The simulator used to get this backwards, which is why the previous version of this guide overstated how far ahead the Treasure plan is — see below.
  • Play Figurines before the money they might draw, and Pendant last. Figurine draws two cards; discarding an Action is optional and gives one coin and one Buy. Pendant counts the different Treasure names already in play, including Pendant itself. Additional copies do not add names, but each copy still pays the full count.
  • Keep Watchtower in hand when its reaction matters. It can trash a newly gained Curse or topdeck a useful gain. Once played, it cannot provide that hand reaction. Figurine’s discard also gives up the reaction for that turn; decline it when protection or topdecking is worth more than the coin and Buy. The recorded policy always discards the cheapest available Action, breaking equal-cost ties by name.

Card timing follows the Plunder rulebook and the Rising Sun rulebook. The sequencing recommendation is an inference from those rules and the local comparisons.

Catapult, Credit and Museum

Catapult is a real threat, even though this policy never buys one. Trashing Copper forces discards; trashing Silver gives both a Curse and a discard attack. Trashing Silk Merchant gives a Curse and also returns a Coffers and a Villager to its owner. Watchtower can intercept the Curse if held in hand. A Catapult opponent may justify a more defensive or adaptive plan than these fixed policies.

Rocks has no printed victory points. Gaining or trashing it gains a Silver onto your deck during your Buy phase, otherwise into your hand. Catapult and Rocks count as two different Museum names but one supply pile for game-ending purposes. See the Empires rules.

Credit buys timing at a premium: pay $2 to gain an Action or Treasure costing at most $8, then take debt equal to its cost. It cannot gain Platinum ($9), Colony, or Province. Getting a needed card now can help a weak opening, but the debt competes with your next purchases. The chosen policy won without using it; that does not establish that Credit is never correct. See the Credit card and debt rules.

Museum adds two points for each different card name you own. Your first Colony is therefore worth 12 total points, first Province 8, first Duchy 5, and a replacement first Estate 3 if you previously trashed all Estates. A missing Action or Treasure is worth 2, and your first Curse is worth a net 1. Collect these near the end; buying weak cards too early can cost more in tempo. Check the score before emptying Colonies, Provinces or a third pile. The simulator’s shared ending guard avoids purchases that immediately end the game without a lead.

What the experiments establish

A screen scored 240 policy specifications against two opponents, 100 games each (48,000 games). One specification, a mutual-Catapult trashing plan whose games could stall past the turn cap, was disqualified rather than tolerated; no comparison below contains a truncated game. The surviving top eight joined five baselines in a 13-policy round robin, 300 games per pairing (23,400 games).

A four-round iterated coordinate ascent then swept every field of the policy class from the round robin’s leader, each round restarting from the previous round’s best, and converged (174,000 games over 290 specifications). Its top five, the round-robin leader, the previously published policy and three baselines — ten policies — then played a full round robin at 3,000 games per pairing (135,000 games) to choose the winner, rather than freezing a single sweep result. The winner was then re-measured on fresh seeds against every other entrant (36,000 games), against the three strongest engine finalists (12,000 games), and against itself with one field reverted at a time (56,000 games).

Total retained evidence: 488,400 games. Every seed is played in both starting seats; ties earn half a win, and equal scores use the fewer-turns tiebreaker. All retained games reached normal endings.

What changed since the previous version, and why

This guide replaces one published on September 17, 2026. Three of its claims did not survive review.

  • The engine gap was overstated. The simulator’s Action ordering played Watchtower — a terminal that draws to six — ahead of Stables and Innkeeper, so any engine hand holding both burned its last Action on the Watchtower and stranded the rest. Replaying that exact pairing with the order corrected gives 65.8% (95% interval 64.3%–67.2%, 4,000 games) in place of the 72.3% previously published. The correction cannot cost the money deck anything, because it only reorders cards that refund an Action and this deck buys none of them — no Stables, Harbor Village, Innkeeper or Conclave. That was confirmed before the old behaviour was removed: the corrected and uncorrected versions of the previous policy were identical in all 4,000 paired games played against each other.
  • Conclave could not be aimed. BaseAI.choose_action_to_play_with_conclave defined a default but never consulted the strategy, so no policy could choose what Conclave plays. The default ranks candidates by printed +Cards, which is zero for Watchtower and Stables — the only two cards on this board that draw. It is now a live hook.
  • The search could not reach its own answer. The previous refine stage was a single coordinate sweep from one base and never varied money, silvers, golds, platinums or province_at, so every policy it ever scored was pinned to two Platinums, one Gold and two Silvers. That both capped what any engine could pay for and hid the Gold-first buy order, which needs two fields to move at once. The sweep is now iterated to convergence over every field, and the winner is chosen by a round robin instead of frozen from one noisy sweep — the earlier rule picked a policy that then lost to the one it replaced.

Rules audit and reproduction

Before retaining these comparisons, the simulator was corrected so Pendant pays when played; Figurine draws on play and conditionally gives a coin and Buy; Watchtower draws only up to six; consecutive and indirectly played Harbor Villages consume the next-Action trigger correctly; and Rocks has its proper type, scoring and Silver gain/trash effects. The two play-skill corrections above are covered by regression tests as well. This was a focused audit, not certification of every expansion interaction.
