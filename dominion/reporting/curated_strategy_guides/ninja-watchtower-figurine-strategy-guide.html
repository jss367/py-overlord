Ninja, Watchtower
and Figurine

Build a compact Action package and let Treasure-phase draw carry the economy. The selected policy buys three Figurines before emphasizing money; two Figurines performed equally well in the final comparison.

Recommendation: open Ninja / Watchtower on a $4/$3 split, collect roughly three Figurines, then Gold, Pendant and Platinum. Buy Colonies when you can and use Museum diversity to finish. The broader search strongly favored this family over the tested Stables and Silk Merchant engines.

Your board

Catapult/Rocks · Watchtower · Conclave · Harbor Village · Innkeeper · Ninja · Silk Merchant · Figurine · Pendant · Stables.

Landscapes: Credit and Museum. Base piles: Platinum and Colony included. Assumptions: two players, seven Coppers and three Estates each, no Shelters or additional landscapes. Catapult is the standard split pile: five Catapults above five Rocks.

How to play it

1. Small Action package

One Ninja and one Watchtower. Ninja pressures the opponent; Watchtower restores a small hand after an attack. Avoid filling the deck with competing terminal Actions.

2. Draw with Treasures

Build toward three Figurines. They draw during the Treasure phase without spending Actions. Buy Silver on small turns, then Gold, Pendant and Platinum as the deck develops.

3. Score and diversify

Take Colonies at $11. Add Provinces as the game advances and take useful missing card names for Museum when they do not cost a stronger scoring buy.

Opening details: on $4/$3 or $3/$4, buy Ninja on the $4 turn and Watchtower on the $3 turn. The exact tested policy also buys Ninja on a $5/$2 split and passes at $2. Credit openings were explored, but the selected policy does not use Credit. A dedicated search conditioned on each opening split was not performed.

The caps describe the reproducible policy, not a requirement to buy every target. Platinum and Colonies often arrive before all the Gold or Pendant targets are reached. The policy normally skips early Catapult, Conclave, Harbor Village, Innkeeper, Silk Merchant and Stables; it may collect their first copies late for Museum.

Flexible choices: the three-Figurine policy scored 50.2% against two Figurines (95% interval 47.4–53.0%) and 48.8% against adding one early Silk Merchant (45.8–51.8%). Neither result establishes a meaningful advantage. Treat two to three Figurines as the target range, with an early Silk Merchant a competitive option.

Turn sequencing that matters

  • Watchtower versus Ninja: with only one Action, you often must choose. The tested policy plays Watchtower first when the hand has five or fewer cards, otherwise Ninja. It does not assume it can play both every turn. Ninja may be played from the deck, but still consumes an Action. Watchtower does not block Ninja’s discard attack.
  • Play Figurines before the money they might draw, and Pendant last. Figurine draws two cards; discarding an Action is optional and gives one coin and one Buy. Extra Figurines can draw other Figurines and Treasures. Pendant counts the different Treasure names already in play, including Pendant itself. Additional copies do not add names.
  • Keep Watchtower in hand when its reaction matters. It can trash a newly gained Curse or topdeck a useful gain. Once played, it cannot provide that hand reaction. Figurine’s discard also gives up the reaction for that turn; decline it when protection or topdecking is worth more than the coin and Buy. This is advice for adaptive play: the recorded policy always discards the cheapest available Action, breaking equal-cost ties by name. The simulator exposes a strategy hook for choosing another Action or declining, but that alternative policy was not included in the recorded results.
  • If you pivot into Actions: Harbor Village supplies Actions; putting Conclave or Catapult immediately after it also earns its coin bonus. Conclave can play an Action from hand with a name not already in play, then supplies an Action. These are useful tactical options, but the tested larger engines lost to the Treasure plan.

Card timing follows the Plunder rulebook and the Rising Sun rulebook. The sequencing recommendation is an inference from those rules and the local comparisons.

Catapult, Credit and Museum

Catapult is a real threat, even though this policy skips buying it early. Trashing Copper forces discards; trashing Silver gives both a Curse and a discard attack. Trashing Silk Merchant gives a Curse and also returns a Coffers and a Villager to its owner. Watchtower can intercept the Curse if held in hand. A Catapult opponent may justify a more defensive or adaptive plan than these fixed policies.

Rocks has no printed victory points. Gaining or trashing it gains a Silver onto your deck during your Buy phase, otherwise into your hand. Catapult and Rocks count as two different Museum names but one supply pile for game-ending purposes. See the Empires rules.

Credit buys timing at a premium: pay $2 to gain an Action or Treasure costing at most $8, then take debt equal to its cost. It cannot gain Platinum ($9), Colony, or Province. Getting a needed card now can help a weak opening, but the debt competes with your next purchases. The chosen policy won without using it; that does not establish that Credit is never correct. See the Credit card and debt rules.

Museum adds two points for each different card name you own. Your first Colony is therefore worth 12 total points, first Province 8, first Duchy 5, and a replacement first Estate 3 if you previously trashed all Estates. A missing Action or Treasure is worth 2, and your first Curse is worth a net 1. Collect these near the end; buying weak cards too early can cost more in tempo. Check the score before emptying Colonies, Provinces or a third pile. The simulator’s shared ending guard avoids purchases that immediately end the game without a lead.

What the experiments establish

The initial screen tested 72 policy specifications against two opponents, 40 games each (5,760 games). The top eight joined four baselines in a 12-policy round robin, 160 games per pairing (10,560 games). The six-Figurine money baseline won that tournament with a 79.7% score rate across its 11 opponents.

A subsequent local refinement compared 42 specifications against that winner and the strongest Stables engine, 100 games per pairing (8,400 games). Three Figurines was selected before the final validation. The frozen policy then faced eight opponents for 1,000 fresh games each (8,000 games). Total retained evidence: 32,720 games. Every seed is played in both starting seats; ties earn half a win, and equal scores use the fewer-turns tiebreaker. All retained games reached normal endings.

Rules audit and reproduction

Before retaining these comparisons, the simulator was corrected so Pendant pays when played; Figurine draws on play and conditionally gives a coin and Buy; Watchtower draws only up to six; consecutive and indirectly played Harbor Villages consume the next-Action trigger correctly; and Rocks has its proper type, scoring and Silver gain/trash effects. Regression tests cover these board interactions. This was a focused audit, not certification of every expansion interaction.
