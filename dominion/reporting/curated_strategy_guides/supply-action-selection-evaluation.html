Supply Action decision evaluation · October 2026

Choosing Supply Actions

Captain and Band of Misfits now have independent strategy target choices, and all three Commands share a deterministic tactical fallback. The tested policy improved Captain and Overlord against two opponents, produced smaller uncertain gains for Band of Misfits, and had no measured regressions in nine comparisons.

The 3,600-game study measures decisions in the current simulator. Known scheduling and indirect-play defects limit conclusions about actual Dominion strength. Duration valuation has scenario evidence only.

Using the target choices

Captain uses captain_target_priority; Band of Misfits uses band_of_misfits_target_priority. Each is an ordered list of conditional PriorityRule entries. Their hand action_priority remains independent. A Python strategy may also override choose_captain_target(state, player, choices) or choose_band_of_misfits_target(state, player, choices).

strategy.action_priority = [PriorityRule("Village"), PriorityRule("Smithy")]
strategy.captain_target_priority = [
    PriorityRule("Chapel", lambda state, player:
        sum(card.name == "Curse" for card in player.hand) >= 2),
    PriorityRule("Smithy"),
]
strategy.band_of_misfits_target_priority = [PriorityRule("Militia")]

The example illustrates separate preferences; it is not an optimized strategy. Overlord retains its existing hand and phase preferences for compatibility, followed by the shared supply baseline; overriding choose_overlord_target can still choose a different payload.

All three supply plays are mandatory when a legal target exists. An override returning None, an unavailable card, or an invalid object requests the shared fallback. An empty menu does nothing. Failed conditional rules prefer unspecified targets, but the baseline still chooses when every rule fails. Captain reevaluates its next-turn choice against the new hand and current costs. Dedicated priorities are Python strategy settings, not new optimizer genes.

What the baseline considers

Needed Action support comes first when terminal Actions in hand exceed remaining Actions. Next comes available draw and money, attack pressure, bounded junk removal, and discounted next-turn resources. Draw is capped by the deck plus discard; ties use Actions, Buys, printed coin cost and card name rather than Supply order.

Militia's attack estimate changes with opposing hand sizes. Witch, Sea Hag and Familiar lose their cursing premium when Curses run out. Chapel, Steward and Junk Dealer receive hand-dependent trashing estimates; mandatory Junk Dealer trash is penalized in a clean hand. Starting Copper economy is preserved until other printed Treasure income reaches $3. Small Victory cards stop counting as junk at two remaining Provinces.

Caravan, Fishing Village, Wharf, Merchant Ship and Lighthouse have explicit current and future resource estimates. Future value is discounted by 25% and ignored at two remaining Provinces. Pillage and Feast are penalized because a virtual target cannot trash itself for its payoff. Unmodeled effects use printed resources and a coarse Attack premium.

Fixed-seed, seat-balanced comparisons

Each row uses 100 distinct seeds, both seats for each seed, and both policies: 200 games per policy, 400 games per row. Policies share purchases and hand sequencing. Each seed is reset before every seat/policy combination. Opponents are the registered Smithy money, Village/Laboratory engine and Chapel/Witch strategies.

The probe buys Provinces, late Duchies, up to two copies of its Command, Gold, one Smithy, Silver and late Estates, in that order. Its only explicit hand Action rule is the Command; normal fallback still plays the purchased Smithy. Trash priorities remove Curses, non-endgame Estates and Coppers backed by $3 of other printed Treasure income. This exposes the supply fallback rather than an optimized dedicated target policy.

Each kingdom, in Supply insertion order, is Chapel, Village, Smithy, Militia, Witch, Laboratory, Market, Festival, Moat and the row's Command. Legacy Captain uses hand selection then the first target; legacy Band of Misfits uses its old printed draw/Actions/cost ranking; legacy Overlord uses existing preferences then its old printed-resource fallback. Both policies run on the current legal menus and rules engine. The study therefore isolates decision changes rather than measuring the earlier cost/menu defects.

The half-width is 27.2 percentage points; these bounds are deliberately conservative and remain nonzero with no discordant outcomes. Wins use the simulator's winner selector, which breaks equal scores using turns taken and then seat order. Alternating seats controls that bias. These are per-row bounds, without correction for nine simultaneous comparisons, and measure seed sampling only.

Captain's gain against Smithy money and Overlord's gains against Smithy money and Chapel/Witch clear these conservative bounds. Captain's smaller attack-opponent gain and both Band of Misfits gains remain uncertain. The Village/Laboratory opponent loses every game to both versions, so those rows do not distinguish the policies. No observed regression proves neither universal improvement nor optimal play; different purchases, hand rules, opponents, pile ordering for legacy Captain, and defensive cards can change the result.

Remaining rules defects and limits

The audit separately filed Captain next-turn scheduling and repeated plays (#395), Overlord and Band of Misfits indirect Action handling (#396), and virtual Supply proxies and Duration owner tracking (#397). Five strict expected-failure tests reproduce them.

Captain currently renews its effect beyond the next turn and deduplicates repeated initial plays. Overlord and Band of Misfits bypass Action counters, Ways and other centralized indirect-play handling, including Enlightenment substitutions. Virtual Duration targets can be counted as owned, and Overlord temporarily puts its target in play. Matches exclude Duration payloads, but both Captain policies retain the existing scheduling defect. Absolute strength remains provisional; rerun these comparisons after those fixes.

The policy is a small, explicit heuristic. It does not interpret all card text, value every nested choice or reaction, or score resource substitutions under Enlightenment. Attack defense, multiplayer scaling, Colony endings and exact deck composition are not modeled. The endgame horizon uses remaining Provinces rather than predicting the end of the game.

Current rules distinguish playing a card from becoming it and specify that a played virtual target cannot move itself out of play. See the designer's play-without-moving clarification and the publisher's Captain rules. Current non-Command restrictions are reflected in the audited menus.
