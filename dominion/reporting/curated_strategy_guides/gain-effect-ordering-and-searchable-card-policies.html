The current engine differs from this retained benchmark snapshot. Later gain-movement and off-turn prophecy corrections are covered by synthetic regressions; these saved games have not been regraded or relabeled as results from the later engine.

What changes during a gain

The real turn player resolves their effects first, followed by other owners in turn order. Each owner chooses one offered effect with choose_gain_effect(state, player, gained_card, choices). Choices are objects with kind, source and a readable label. Return an offered object or its kind string; invalid results use the first legal choice. Existing card-specific decisions still decide whether to react. Returning no ordering choice does not decline all effects.

Active instructions are captured when the gain occurs. Newly drawn hand reactions can join after each effect. Each physical hand reaction is offered at most once per gain, and the selected instruction is consumed before nested gains run. A 256-effect guard raises an error for pathological custom chains. Once-per-event instructions, gain counts, gain-time costs and types are reserved before nested effects can change them. The choice hook and resolution both run with the effect owner's current-player context while preserving the real turn player.

Sleigh can resolve before or after Sheepdog. Additional Sleighs can be discarded for their discard effects even after the gained card stops moving. Watchtower, Cargo Ship, Innovation, Deliver, Sailor and other movers respect the original destination. Moving a gain to another zone prevents later movers from following it; topdecking a card already on top of its original deck does not change zones. Covering a gained deck card stops its movement even if a nested gain subsequently removes the covering card. Landing Party instructions cannot recreate a trashed physical card or a virtual Supply proxy.

Regression scenarios include both Sleigh/Sheepdog orders, newly drawn reactions, multiple Sleigh discards with nested gains, Watchtower movement, covered deck cards, Cargo Ship competition, off-turn ownership, gain counters and reserved instructions, live type changes, Landing Party source ownership and copied player-ID maps. This is a scoped audit of the common event path, not certification of every Dominion interaction.

Later synthetic regressions cover both Sleigh orders against Progress, Rapid Expansion and Changeling; Harsh Winter off-turn Debt; and gain-time Gatekeeper type/Exile qualification. Changeling cannot return a gain that already moved, and its replacement always enters discard, including when the original gain was to hand or deck. This corrects an older topdeck-position test that contradicted the publisher's Nocturne exchange rules. The Rising Sun rules restrict Harsh Winter to the gainer's actual turn. These rule corrections are not new tactical comparisons.

The designer's stop-moving clarification defines expected gain locations and covered deck cards. The publisher's Menagerie rules explain newly drawn Sheepdog reactions; the Empires rules explain simultaneous effects across owners.

Investment counts the actual remaining hand

Investment first trashes a hand card, then chooses +$1 or self-trash. Self-trash awards one point per differently named live Treasure remaining in hand; Investment is not a second selected hand Treasure. The decision and scoring use the same live-type helper, including Capitalism, Charlatan and Inheritance interactions. If Investment cannot trash itself, the mode remains money.

The corrected inherited strategy compares actual points with a conservative threshold of three names, discounted to two when the weakest live Treasure name is repeated. Copper is the weakest name when present; otherwise printed cost and name break ties. This preserves the legacy decisions on ordinary Treasure hands without pretending to remove a hand card. Tests exhaust ordinary Copper/Silver/Gold/Investment multisets through six cards. Explicit mode overrides still take precedence. The old Treasure-selection method remains callable for compatibility and is documented as unused by the card engine; the historical keyword spelling of the self-trash flag remains accepted.

Counting two real points does not by itself prove that cashing out now is best. The screen separately varies a uniform three-name rule, two-name rule, buy-breakpoint protection, Treasure play order, mandatory hand-trash priorities, future junk retention, excessive-copy cash-out and closing points. The latter three are separate ablations; they are not bundled with the hand-trash change. The corrected compatibility policy was the predeclared production candidate, locked before fresh validation. No diagnostic late-only policy is promoted.

Boards, selection and uncertainty

The Investment and optimizer panels use Watchtower, Clerk, Investment, Bounty Hunter, Village, Smithy, Laboratory, Witch, Militia and Moat, standard starting decks and no landscapes. The three opponents are Smithy money, a Village/Laboratory engine and a Clerk/Witch attack. The engine is weak on this board and lacks its preferred Chapel. Investment purchases and play order follow the historical fixed subject: Province, Gold, up to two Investments, one Smithy, late Duchy, Silver, late Estate; Investment plays before Gold, Silver and Copper. Hand trash prefers Curse, eligible Estate, then Copper above $4 owned printed Treasure income. Only the named hand-trash or play-order ablation changes those choices.

The optimizer adds up to one Clerk, Bounty Hunter and Watchtower, with Action order Bounty Hunter, Clerk, Watchtower, Smithy. Eight candidates receive 24 calls each to production tactical mutation with a fixed local random seed. The unconfigured baseline is also evaluated. Purchase genes do not vary. Selection maximizes equal-weight average screen win share among mutated candidates, with deterministic tie-breaking. This is a bounded mutation screen that exercises production genes, not a full evolutionary purchase-and-policy tournament.

Investment uses 30 screen seeds and 100 fresh validation seeds, both seats. The optimizer uses 20 screen seeds and 100 fresh validation seeds. Validation loads the frozen screen hash and rejects changed inputs or overlapping seeds. Wins score 1, ties ½, losses 0, with fewer turns taken breaking equal physical scores. Both seats are averaged within each independent seed block. Conservative 95% Hoeffding bounds for paired win-share changes have radius ±27.16 percentage points at 100 seeds (±49.59 at 30; ±60.74 at 20). Score-change intervals are paired normal approximations. All are per-comparison and exploratory, without multiplicity adjustment. Equal initial seeds can diverge after different decisions.
